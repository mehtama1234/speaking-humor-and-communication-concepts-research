Calm nerves with practice.&nbsp;
Have you ever been in front of an audience&nbsp;
and had trouble letting go of a lectern?&nbsp;&nbsp;
Nervous mannerisms can mean trouble for&nbsp;
speakers. For example, shifting your weight,&nbsp;&nbsp;
adjusting your glasses, wringing your hands.&nbsp;
These are all things you might do that lessen&nbsp;&nbsp;
the impact of your presentations and, oh!&nbsp;
Tugging at your clothes; that's a problem, too.&nbsp;
You'd be surprised how nervous tics and&nbsp;
inconsistent body language can distract&nbsp;&nbsp;
an audience. Gestures that don t coordinate&nbsp;
well with a speaker's message can drain the&nbsp;&nbsp;
speaker's energy and shift the audience's&nbsp;
focus away from the intended message.&nbsp;
So, what should a speaker do? The&nbsp;
answer is planning and practice.&nbsp;
The more you rehearse your speech, the better idea&nbsp;
you'll have for gestures that emphasize a point.&nbsp;&nbsp;
For example, if you want to explain to a group&nbsp;
that your new home is very far from your family,&nbsp;&nbsp;
you could extend one arm out from your&nbsp;
body while placing your other hand on your&nbsp;&nbsp;
heart to demonstrate the distance. This&nbsp;
descriptive gesture conveys the message&nbsp;&nbsp;
of distance while suggesting the idea&nbsp;
that your home is far from your heart.&nbsp;
Knowing that you're going to perform this&nbsp;
gesture at a targeted moment in your speech&nbsp;&nbsp;
will guide you as you prepare. When you&nbsp;
perform planned gestures in a speech,&nbsp;&nbsp;
you'll be able to channel nervous energy into&nbsp;
constructive movements that support your ideas.&nbsp;
Types of gestures
What kinds of&nbsp;&nbsp;
gestures should you imagine when planning a&nbsp;
speech? Think of them in four major groups.&nbsp;
First, descriptive gestures , like the one&nbsp;
I just used, help make your words clearer.&nbsp;&nbsp;
They can help describe things like the size,&nbsp;
shape, and location of objects you're discussing.
Second, emblematic gestures&nbsp;
are culturally specific,&nbsp;&nbsp;
though some are universal&nbsp;
such as nodding and shrugging.&nbsp;
Emblematic movements underscore strong&nbsp;
emotion such as earnestness and conviction.&nbsp;&nbsp;
In many cultures placing your hand on your heart&nbsp;
communicates a powerful belief in your message.
Third, representational gestures include movements&nbsp;
that give information about what you re doing.&nbsp;&nbsp;
You might use your hands to demonstrate&nbsp;
chopping, throwing, or picking something up.
And fourth, pantomime gestures are a good way&nbsp;
to ask your audience to participate or respond.&nbsp;&nbsp;
If you want them to raise their hands, for&nbsp;
example, you may raise your hand to show them.&nbsp;
Well, that's a lot of gestures, isn't&nbsp;
it? And that's good. For all the choices,&nbsp;&nbsp;
you can select those gestures that&nbsp;
naturally fit what you want to say.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Start by thinking about the ways you normally&nbsp;
move and try to rehearse those movements that come&nbsp;&nbsp;
naturally for you. If you feel you have to force&nbsp;
something that doesn't come from your instinctive&nbsp;&nbsp;
whole-body movements, think of another gesture or&nbsp;
movement that feels more natural to your style.&nbsp;
Movements such as this
are probably not expressive&nbsp;&nbsp;

Try to focus your thoughts on the words&nbsp;

Consider movements that are a natural response&nbsp;

A word of caution when planning your gestures.&nbsp;
Some gestures that are perfectly appropriate&nbsp;&nbsp;

gestures
well.&nbsp;
Coordination
Coordinating gestures, eye contact, and&nbsp;
movement can be a challenge for any speaker.&nbsp;&nbsp;
When you present to an in-person audience, you&nbsp;
will need to practice making eye contact while&nbsp;&nbsp;
walking across a stage. It is another set of&nbsp;
skills entirely to coordinate gestures and&nbsp;&nbsp;
make eye contact while meeting fully online&nbsp;
or with virtual attendees and an in-person&nbsp;&nbsp;
audience during a hybrid meeting. Those skills&nbsp;
take time to develop in front of your webcam.&nbsp;
No matter how you meet in-person, fully online,&nbsp;
or hybrid take the time to practice with your&nbsp;&nbsp;
equipment or arrive early and walk around.
With your planned and rehearsed gestures,&nbsp;&nbsp;
you'll be able to let go of the lectern in no&nbsp;
