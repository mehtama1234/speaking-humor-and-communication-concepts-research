One of the most demanding situations you'll ever&nbsp;
face is speaking to an audience, and it can be&nbsp;&nbsp;
daunting thinking up topics for your speech. My&nbsp;
involvement in Toastmasters has taught me how to&nbsp;&nbsp;
find speech inspiration everywhere, and I m going&nbsp;

&nbsp;
One of the most common fears is the fear of&nbsp;
public speaking, and one reason people dread&nbsp;&nbsp;
it is because they picture themselves at a loss&nbsp;
for words. It can be terrifying, but Toastmasters&nbsp;&nbsp;
knows that speech topics are all around us you&nbsp;
just need to learn how to recognize them.
&nbsp;
The best place to start looking for&nbsp;
speech ideas is within yourself.&nbsp;&nbsp;
You're an interesting person. Did you know that?
Think about your personal experience.
&nbsp;
A few specific areas in your life might&nbsp;
hold the most interesting speech topics.&nbsp;&nbsp;
They might be your interests, your career, your&nbsp;
family, or your education. What you do day-to-day,&nbsp;&nbsp;
such as your job or your favorite activities&nbsp;
or hobbies, can offer a lot of choices:&nbsp;&nbsp;
do you work with interesting people in an&nbsp;
exciting setting, or do something unique?
&nbsp;
What can you tell people about your family?&nbsp;
Do you have secrets for getting along?&nbsp;&nbsp;
How would you describe your education? Did,&nbsp;
or do, you attend a remarkable school?
&nbsp;
Of course, there are many more fascinating topics&nbsp;
in your life, all you need to do is share them.&nbsp;&nbsp;
For example, what adventures have you been on?&nbsp;
Have you climbed a mountain or won a game show?&nbsp;&nbsp;
What challenges have you faced in your life?&nbsp;
Have you cared for a person who is ill?&nbsp;&nbsp;
What kind of friends do you have? Do you know&nbsp;
people involved in interesting careers or other&nbsp;&nbsp;
activities? What's your favorite meal, and if&nbsp;
you prepare it yourself, what's the recipe?
&nbsp;
I know you could spend some time&nbsp;
answering any one of these questions.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Of course, talking about&nbsp;
yourself is just the beginning.
&nbsp;
Check reference materials.
When you're looking for topics,&nbsp;&nbsp;
there are four major resources that can&nbsp;
help you: websites, newspapers, books,&nbsp;&nbsp;
and magazines are all packed with stories and&nbsp;
facts you can use to make great speeches.
&nbsp;
Let's say you've never traveled to&nbsp;
Canada, but you've always wanted to;&nbsp;&nbsp;
there's plenty you can do to prepare for a&nbsp;
speech about the trip you would like to take.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Visit websites for the country, provinces,&nbsp;
territories, cities, and tourist attractions&nbsp;&nbsp;
that you would like to visit. Think of&nbsp;
how you can describe these in your speech.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Check news sources for stories about Canada&nbsp;
and the regions that you plan to visit. Visit&nbsp;&nbsp;
the library or the bookstore and look at&nbsp;
books and travel magazines for more ideas.
&nbsp;
Research can help you sound like a travel agent&nbsp;
and sell your trip to your listeners. Offer&nbsp;&nbsp;
tips that they can't resist. Look up topics like:&nbsp;
What are the best deals? What are the most amazing&nbsp;&nbsp;
sights? How would you travel from one place to&nbsp;
another, and how could you go on a small budget?&nbsp;&nbsp;
These are only a few ideas that you can find&nbsp;
when reading through reference materials.
&nbsp;
You'll have so many things to talk&nbsp;
about, it could present a problem.&nbsp;&nbsp;
So how do you choose from all the information?
&nbsp;
Focus on your audience s needs.
You might have some big ideas in mind,&nbsp;&nbsp;
but they seem too broad for any one&nbsp;
speech. Knowing your audience can&nbsp;&nbsp;
solve this problem, because you can focus on&nbsp;
the information that they need to hear.
&nbsp;
If you're speaking to a large audience&nbsp;
composed of international business experts,&nbsp;&nbsp;
focus your speech on the topics that&nbsp;
will engage that particular group.&nbsp;&nbsp;
For example, instead of talking about Canada&nbsp;
as a great vacation destination in general,&nbsp;&nbsp;
you could center your talk on the&nbsp;
economic benefits of the country.&nbsp;&nbsp;
On the other hand, if your audience is a small&nbsp;
group of sports enthusiasts, you could describe&nbsp;&nbsp;
the Canadian ski trip of your dreams.
Other factors you might want to include could&nbsp;&nbsp;
be the audience's age range, education,&nbsp;
interests, and shared experiences.
&nbsp;
Recognize the occasion
Another way to narrow&nbsp;&nbsp;
down your topic is to make it relevant to the&nbsp;
occasion or event where you'll be speaking.
&nbsp;
For example, if the event has a special theme, you&nbsp;
could concentrate on that. Let's say you've been&nbsp;&nbsp;
asked to speak about a business benefitting you,&nbsp;
your family, or an organization important to you.&nbsp;&nbsp;
You could research the company to&nbsp;
learn more about it in general. Then,&nbsp;&nbsp;
keeping it in line with your event, you could&nbsp;
speak about ways everyone can benefit from the&nbsp;&nbsp;
products or services offered by the business.
As you can see, you can easily narrow your&nbsp;&nbsp;
topic by audience and event, but there's&nbsp;
still one more way to narrow it down.
&nbsp;
Are you qualified to speak on a topic?
&nbsp;
While you can probably speak on just about any&nbsp;
topic with the proper research, there might be&nbsp;&nbsp;
limitations to consider. First, if the audience is&nbsp;
composed of experts in a field who are expecting&nbsp;&nbsp;
a speech from one of their peers, you may not&nbsp;
qualify to cover some topics for that group.
&nbsp;
For example, your audience may be a group of&nbsp;
lawyers who are interested in receiving new&nbsp;&nbsp;
information on personal injury lawsuits. If you're&nbsp;
not a lawyer, you may not want to lecture them on&nbsp;&nbsp;
case law. However, if you were in a car accident,&nbsp;
and experienced the effects of a particular kind&nbsp;&nbsp;
of injury law, you might have a powerful topic&nbsp;
to speak about. You could describe your own&nbsp;&nbsp;
experiences, including the challenges and rewards&nbsp;
that came your way in the legal system.
&nbsp;
Of course, a lot depends on&nbsp;
how you address the material,&nbsp;&nbsp;
and ultimately you might win over any audience&nbsp;
with any topic if you have enough enthusiasm and&nbsp;&nbsp;
knowledge to capture their interest.
With these details, you can take command&nbsp;&nbsp;
of your speeches. And remember,&nbsp;
topics are everywhere.
