&nbsp;
When friends gather to celebrate, some occasions&nbsp;
are more formal than others, but even a formal&nbsp;&nbsp;
occasion doesn't have to be intimidating. The&nbsp;
first tip will help you succeed with any toast.
&nbsp;
One: Keep it short and sweet
One skill that Toastmasters learn is how to&nbsp;&nbsp;
get to the point. With a toast, this is especially&nbsp;
important. You want to stand up, raise your glass,&nbsp;&nbsp;
and say a few words to make everyone smile, but&nbsp;
do it in a way that is brief and engaging.
&nbsp;
Since you're keeping it to a few&nbsp;
minutes, what should you talk about?&nbsp;&nbsp;
That might depend on the situation.
Toasts happen at all types of events, not&nbsp;&nbsp;
just weddings. For example, many memorial services&nbsp;
reserve time for guests to honor the departed.
&nbsp;
For a eulogy, it's a good idea to focus on&nbsp;
celebrating the positive and most important&nbsp;&nbsp;
parts of that person's life. Highlight a few&nbsp;
remarkable things that they accomplished,&nbsp;&nbsp;
but make it a short list. Platitudes,&nbsp;
cliches, or long chronologies may not&nbsp;&nbsp;
only bore some listeners; they will&nbsp;
steal the impact of your message.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Instead, try to express what gave that&nbsp;
person joy and a sense of accomplishment.
&nbsp;
For a wedding, speak from the&nbsp;
heart, and wish the couple well.&nbsp;&nbsp;
You might mention one aspect of their relationship&nbsp;
that you admire, but be sure it's appropriate.
&nbsp;
Two: Be Careful with humor
&nbsp;
Humor has a place in every toast, regardless of&nbsp;
the setting, just remember to be empathetic to&nbsp;&nbsp;
the honoree and the circumstances. For a wedding&nbsp;
toast, avoid potentially embarrassing stories.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Risqu jokes or anecdotes as a part of a&nbsp;
toast for any celebration can be hazardous.&nbsp;&nbsp;
You're better off if you keep the focus&nbsp;
on the person or the event being honored,&nbsp;&nbsp;
and not on stories the couple, their guests,&nbsp;
or other honoree might find uncomfortable.
&nbsp;
Three: Practice
&nbsp;
When your goal is a great toast, practice&nbsp;
at least once or twice before the big event.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Rehearse the words you'd like to&nbsp;
say, but also plan an opening.&nbsp;&nbsp;
To begin, stand up, raise your glass,&nbsp;
and say, I'd like to propose a toast.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Then, practice giving your fellow celebrants&nbsp;
time to raise their glasses before you speak.
&nbsp;
Also, you may not have access to a microphone, so&nbsp;
if you'll be addressing a large gathering, it's&nbsp;&nbsp;
a good idea to practice projecting your voice.&nbsp;
Don't yell, but breathe deeply, and let your voice&nbsp;&nbsp;
radiate from your center. Practice will help.
With a little preparation and practice, you can&nbsp;&nbsp;
master the art of toasting and help make every&nbsp;
celebration an event to remember. Cheers!
