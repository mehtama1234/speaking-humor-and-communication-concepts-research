# Speaking Practice Case Studies

Concrete examples of different learners using the repo's speaking stack across
real situations over a few days.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The repo already has:

- real speaker models
- worked speaking examples
- drill cards
- context-first guides
- problem-first recipes

This file adds one more practical layer:

- what it looks like when a learner actually uses the stack over several days

These are not claims about guaranteed outcomes. They are realistic usage
examples showing how a person might move from problem to rep to live attempt.

If you already know the learner pattern but not the exact move being trained,
start with `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` first,
then use these cases to see how that move can be practiced across a few days.

## How to use this file

1. Find the case closest to your real problem.
2. Notice which files the learner uses and in what order.
3. Match it to one item from
   `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`.
4. Borrow the sequence, not the exact words.
5. Use the matching practice recipe or drill card yourself within the week.

## Case 1: Product manager with messy project updates

### Starting problem

She knows the work well, but her updates sound cluttered. She gives too much
background before the point, and people leave unclear on the actual risk or
decision.

Exact approach focus:

- `Approach 5: Prepare With Goal, Audience, And Constraints`
- `Approach 13: Pre-Handle Objections Before The Room Raises Them`

### Files she uses

1. `analysis/business-communication-curriculum.md`
2. `analysis/real-speaker-exemplars.md`
   Focus on:
   - Guy Kawasaki
   - Vinh Giang
3. `analysis/speaking-move-deployment-examples.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Example 3: Guy Kawasaki and next-step persuasion`
   - `Example 5: Matt Abrahams and point-first work communication`
4. `analysis/worked-speaking-situation-examples.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Giving a project update in a meeting`
5. `analysis/speaker-pattern-drill-cards.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Card 2: Bottom line before backstory`
6. `analysis/speaking-pattern-practice-recipes.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Recipe 1`

### What she practices

Day 1:

- records one 60-second update
- notices that the recommendation only appears near the end

Day 2:

- rewrites the same update into:
  - status
  - main risk
  - next step
- records a 45-second version and a 20-second version

Day 3:

- uses the new structure in a real team update:
  - `Quick update: we're on track for Friday, but the main risk is the data pipeline. My recommendation is to cut the low-priority dashboard piece and protect the core release.`

### What changes

- the update sounds more directional
- people ask fewer "wait, so what do you need?" questions
- she feels less pressure to justify every detail

### What to borrow

If this is your problem, copy the sequence:

- speaker model
- before/after example
- short drill
- real meeting update in the same week

## Case 2: Candidate who panics on hard interview questions

### Starting problem

He interviews well on prepared stories but loses shape when a question is
unexpected. He starts too fast and speaks his confusion out loud.

Exact approach focus:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 15: Rehearse The Pressure Shape, Not Just The Content`

### Files he uses

1. `analysis/interview-and-hard-question-curriculum.md`
2. `analysis/real-speaker-exemplars.md`
   Focus on:
   - Sam Altman
   - Matt Abrahams
3. `analysis/speaking-move-deployment-examples.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Example 1: Sam Altman and charged questions`
4. `analysis/worked-speaking-situation-examples.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Answering a hard question when you do not know everything yet`
5. `analysis/speaker-pattern-drill-cards.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Card 3: Honest uncertainty without collapse`
   - `Card 8: First-sentence composure under pressure`
6. `analysis/speaking-pattern-practice-recipes.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Recipe 2`

### What he practices

Day 1:

- writes three hard mock interview questions
- answers all three too quickly on first recording

Day 2:

- repeats them with one full beat before the answer
- uses:
  - what we know
  - what we do not know
  - what happens next

Day 4:

- in a real interview, he gets a question he did not expect and starts with:
  - `The clearest answer is that I don't know the full number yet. What I do know is that the drop started after the onboarding change, and the next thing I'd check is where in the funnel that decline is concentrated.`

### What changes

- the first sentence sounds calmer
- uncertainty sounds more credible
- he no longer burns time describing his own uncertainty

### What to borrow

If hard questions are your issue, protect the first beat. That is often the
whole hinge.

## Case 3: Founder who sounds too defensive in disagreement

### Starting problem

She has a strong view, but when challenged she either hardens too quickly or
over-explains. The disagreement turns into temperature instead of substance.

Exact approach focus:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 19: Take A Position, Give A Reason, Then Reopen`

### Files she uses

1. `analysis/skeptical-room-and-objection-handling-curriculum.md`
2. `analysis/real-speaker-exemplars.md`
   Focus on:
   - Sam Altman
3. `analysis/speaking-move-deployment-examples.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Example 1: Sam Altman and charged questions`
4. `analysis/worked-speaking-situation-examples.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Disagreeing with a stakeholder without sounding defensive`
5. `analysis/speaker-pattern-drill-cards.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Card 1: Acknowledge before you disagree`
6. `analysis/speaking-pattern-practice-recipes.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Recipe 7`

### What she practices

Day 1:

- writes three likely objections from a stakeholder
- answers each one with:
  - acknowledgment
  - difference
  - one reason

Day 3:

- in a real product discussion, she says:
  - `I see the concern. The part I read differently is the cause. I do think we have an adoption issue, but I think it is more setup friction than feature depth.`

### What changes

- the disagreement sounds cleaner
- the other person hears the actual distinction sooner
- the conversation stays more collaborative

### What to borrow

If you are usually too sharp or too vague, this is one of the highest-leverage
patterns in the repo.

## Case 4: Engineer who sounds flat and generic in social conversation

### Starting problem

He is thoughtful one-on-one, but his openers are generic and he defaults to
transactional small talk. He often leaves conversations feeling like he never
really entered them.

Exact approach focus:

- `Approach 8: Use Curiosity To Create Better Conversations`
- `Approach 16: Start With Connection Before Content`

### Files he uses

1. `analysis/social-speaking-and-humor-curriculum.md`
2. `analysis/real-speaker-exemplars.md`
   Focus on:
   - strong conversationalists
3. `analysis/speaking-move-deployment-examples.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Example 8: Curiosity-first warmth in conversation`
4. `analysis/worked-speaking-situation-examples.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Being warmer in small talk without trying to be impressive`
   - `Better follow-up for deeper conversation`
5. `analysis/speaker-pattern-drill-cards.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Card 5: Visible appreciation social opener`
   - `Card 10: Stronger follow-up question for depth`
6. `analysis/speaking-pattern-practice-recipes.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Recipe 4`

### What he practices

Day 1:

- writes three real social openers for people he may actually see this week
- replaces:
  - `How's work?`
  with:
  - `I heard you were the person who got that launch over the line. What ended up being harder about it than people assume?`

Day 3:

- uses one of those openers at a real event

### What changes

- the conversation gets real faster
- the other person offers more detail
- he sounds more interested and less guarded

### What to borrow

If social warmth is your issue, do not aim for "charisma." Aim for specific
attention plus one better follow-up.

## Case 5: Presenter whose openings sound procedural

### Starting problem

She is competent, but her talks begin like calendar invites. The audience
understands the topic, but not why it matters yet.

### Files she uses

1. `analysis/presentation-and-formal-speaking-curriculum.md`
2. `analysis/real-speaker-exemplars.md`
   Focus on:
   - Matt Abrahams
   - Tristan de Montebello
3. `analysis/worked-speaking-situation-examples.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Starting a presentation or answer with more presence`
4. `analysis/speaker-pattern-drill-cards.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Card 7: Moment-based presentation opening`
5. `analysis/speaking-pattern-practice-recipes.md`
   Focus on:
   - `Recipe 6`

### What she practices

Day 1:

- writes one specific moment from a customer incident
- builds a 20-second opening from it

Day 2:

- compares:
  - `Today I want to talk about onboarding`
  against:
  - `Three weeks ago, a customer got stuck before they ever reached the part we were proud of. That's why this matters.`

Day 4:

- uses the stronger opening in rehearsal and keeps it for the live talk

### What changes

- attention arrives faster
- the opening feels more purposeful
- the audience gets the stakes before the details

### What to borrow

If your openings are polite but forgettable, lead with a moment that proves the
point.
