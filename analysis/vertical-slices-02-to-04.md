# Vertical Slices 02–04: Story, Presentation Structure, and Delivery

**Status:** integrated candidate lessons; not independently validated

These slices extend [Vertical Slice 01](vertical-slice-01-awkwardness-repair.md)
from social repair into three other jobs a learner and presentation maker need:
make an idea memorable, orient a room quickly, and sound settled while doing it.

They use the same chain:

```text
source evidence -> shared job -> named move -> practice -> adaptation -> test
```

## Vertical Slice 02 — Make an idea memorable through one embodied moment

### Communication problem

“I have a useful point, but my story becomes summary, biography, or abstract
lesson before the listener can picture anything.”

Shared jobs: `story_or_personal_experience`, `opening`,
`reflection_or_value_frame`, and `callback_or_repetition`.

### Source evidence

- [Ultraspeaking #24: Transform your storytelling by understanding these three layers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GFnmSoMfncw)
  - transcript: `raw-material/youtube/transcripts/ultraspeaking-wave-1/clean/024-GFnmSoMfncw.txt:1-30`
  - existing synthesis: [Evidence 11](/home/mehta/git-repo/speaking-humor-and-communication-concepts-research/analysis/evidence-moments.md#evidence-11-specific-embodied-moments-beat-abstract-storytelling)
- Matthew Dicks story corpus:
  - [session inventory](/home/mehta/git-repo/speaking-humor-and-communication-concepts-research/analysis/matthew-dicks-wave-1-session-inventory.md)
  - [story-worthiness, first-30-seconds, and scene-before-lesson drills](/home/mehta/git-repo/speaking-humor-and-communication-concepts-research/analysis/matthew-dicks-wave-1-drill-candidates.md)
  - exemplar transcripts include `011-TKP8B6fqGsw.txt`, `031-Z1MA_1Hr7OA.txt`, and `050-r8jCcv2QIwM.txt`.

Current candidate move: choose a moment in which something changes, make the
listener able to see/hear the scene, and delay the interpretation until the
scene has done enough work. This is a structure hypothesis, not proof that
every story should be personal or that specificity always improves recall.

### Practice and presentation adaptation

1. List five recent moments; keep the ones containing change, surprise, reveal,
   or collision.
2. Tell one in under two minutes with one visible action, one spoken line, or
   one sensory detail.
3. Do not state the lesson until the listener can describe the moment.
4. For a presentation, open with the scene, then state the principle, then
   return to the audience's problem and next step.

### Validation handoff

- independent reviewer checks whether the excerpt is actually a scene rather
  than a summary;
- recurrence review compares the move across Matthew Dicks, Toastmasters,
  Vinh Giang, and non-story examples;
- listener check asks what changed in the scene and what point the speaker drew;
- non-fit test checks privacy, cultural context, trauma, and whether a story is
  being used as proof for a claim it cannot establish.

## Vertical Slice 03 — Orient a presentation before adding content

### Communication problem

“My opening contains information, but the audience does not yet know why it
should care, where the talk is going, or how the story connects to the point.”

Shared jobs: `opening`, `transition_or_structure`, `definition_or_explanation`,
and `call_to_action_or_audience_role`.

### Source evidence

- [Vinh Giang #17: How to Speak When All Eyes Are On You](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HYNXzKU92Qs)
  - transcript: `raw-material/youtube/transcripts/vinh-giang-wave-1/clean/017-HYNXzKU92Qs.txt:336-391`
  - existing synthesis: [Evidence 10](/home/mehta/git-repo/speaking-humor-and-communication-concepts-research/analysis/evidence-moments.md#evidence-10-story-first-openings-build-connection-before-content)
- Toastmasters exemplar route:
  - [opening-scene and one-moment-story drills](/home/mehta/git-repo/speaking-humor-and-communication-concepts-research/analysis/toastmasters-wave-1-drill-candidates.md)
  - [speech session inventory](/home/mehta/git-repo/speaking-humor-and-communication-concepts-research/analysis/toastmasters-wave-1-session-inventory.md)
- Matthew Dicks: `004-nXoTR5l4Dks.txt` and `048-FOJmSthz8Tk.txt` from the
  [story session inventory](/home/mehta/git-repo/speaking-humor-and-communication-concepts-research/analysis/matthew-dicks-wave-1-session-inventory.md).

Current candidate move: establish connection and direction before loading the
room with content. The ordering may reduce orientation cost, but that is a
mechanism hypothesis until listener comprehension and recall are tested.

### Practice and presentation adaptation

1. Write the opening of an existing talk as a full information summary.
2. Rewrite the first 20 seconds as either a concrete scene, a tension line, or
   a question that names the audience's problem.
3. Add one sentence that states the route: “I’ll show X, then Y, so you can Z.”
4. Check that the opening does not manufacture suspense, overpromise, or hide
   the actual audience role.

Presentation form:

```text
shared situation -> concrete moment or question -> route -> first useful point
```

### Validation handoff

- reviewer labels opening function separately from story quality;
- recurrence compares prepared talks, workplace explanations, and spontaneous
  answers instead of using only polished speeches;
- listener check asks for the talk's purpose and route after the opening;
- non-fit test checks whether a story delays an urgent answer or excludes people
  who do not share the speaker's background.

## Vertical Slice 04 — Use pause and warmth as delivery choices, not identity

### Communication problem

“My content is prepared, but pressure makes me rush, fill silence, overfocus on
words, or sound less settled than I am.”

Shared jobs: `answer_or_response`, `opening`, `transition_or_structure`, and
`qualification_or_boundary`; delivery evidence remains a separate modality
layer rather than a new personality label.

### Source evidence

- [Ultraspeaking #16: How to speak more clearly (without saying a word)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miL69223IOU)
  - transcript: `raw-material/youtube/transcripts/ultraspeaking-wave-1/clean/016-miL69223IOU.txt:237-275`
  - existing synthesis: [Evidence 16](/home/mehta/git-repo/speaking-humor-and-communication-concepts-research/analysis/evidence-moments.md#evidence-16-unhurried-pauses-create-the-feel-of-executive-presence)
- [Vinh Giang #6: How to Be Likeable AND Respected](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sbNc5eL7xNA)
  - transcript: `raw-material/youtube/transcripts/vinh-giang-wave-1/clean/006-sbNc5eL7xNA.txt:49-55`, `:114-123`, `:183-206`
  - existing synthesis: [Evidence 15](/home/mehta/git-repo/speaking-humor-and-communication-concepts-research/analysis/evidence-moments.md#evidence-15-small-vocal-cues-can-shift-authority-without-changing-intelligence)
- [Think Fast Talk Smart #21: Headspace Habits](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GtNid29h70s)
  - transcript: `raw-material/youtube/transcripts/think-fast-talk-smart-wave-1/clean/021-GtNid29h70s.txt:528-624`
  - existing synthesis: [Evidence 17](/home/mehta/git-repo/speaking-humor-and-communication-concepts-research/analysis/evidence-moments.md#evidence-17-presence-degrades-when-content-crowds-out-body-and-voice)

Current candidate move: make time for a thought, reduce filler substitution,
and let delivery support the function of the sentence. Do not label a pause as
confidence, authority, or executive presence without recording the observable
timing and the context in which it occurred.

### Practice and presentation adaptation

1. Answer one question three ways: no pause, one natural pause, and an
   exaggerated pause.
2. Keep the version that separates ideas without breaking the thought.
3. Record a 60-second explanation and mark fillers, rushed transitions, gaze or
   camera changes, and points where content crowded out delivery.
4. Re-record with one change only; do not imitate another speaker's accent,
   pitch, body, gendered mannerism, or status display.

Presentation form:

```text
pause -> bottom line -> one reason -> example or boundary -> next step
```

### Validation handoff

- delivery reviewer records timing, filler, gaze, pace, and context separately;
- proxy measurements remain navigation aids, not interpretations of emotion,
  confidence, trust, or persuasion;
- learner check asks whether the speaker can recognize and revise the delivery
  choice;
- listener check measures comprehension or recall, not whether the listener
  thinks the speaker “looks confident.”
