# Speaking Roleplay Packs

Multi-turn speaking scenarios for practicing not just a first response, but the
follow-up pressure that comes after it.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The repo already has:

- worked before/after examples
- drill cards
- case studies
- real-life simulations

This file adds a different practice layer:

- one realistic prompt
- a common weak answer
- a stronger answer
- one harder follow-up turn
- the move you should protect under that follow-up pressure

This makes practice feel closer to a real exchange instead of a single isolated
answer.

If you know the room but not the exact move to protect under pressure, start
with `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` first, then
use these packs to stress that move across multiple turns.

If you are running these with another person, also use:

- `analysis/partner-roleplay-facilitator-guide.md`

## How to use this file

1. Pick one pack that matches a real situation you expect.
2. Match it to one item from
   `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`.
3. Say the weak version out loud once so you can hear the failure mode.
4. Say the stronger version out loud twice.
5. Answer the escalation turn without reading.
6. Re-run the pack later in the week and compare whether your second-turn answer
   kept its shape.

If you have a partner:

6. Have the facilitator run one cold round, one focused round, and one slightly
   harder round using `analysis/partner-roleplay-facilitator-guide.md`.

## Roleplay 1: Manager asks why a project slipped

### Situation

You are in a project review. A senior person asks:

> "Why did this slip? I thought this was under control."

### Weak first answer

> "Yeah, so there were a lot of moving pieces and design changed some things and engineering had dependencies we were still working through, so it was kind of a complicated situation."

### Stronger first answer

> "The main reason it slipped was the data pipeline dependency. We underestimated how much rework it would create, and that pushed the review by one week. My recommendation now is to cut the low-priority dashboard work and protect the core release."

### Escalation turn

The manager responds:

> "Why wasn't that visible earlier?"

### Good second-turn shape

- answer directly
- own the miss without collapse
- name what changes now

### Stronger second answer

> "We should have surfaced that risk earlier. The miss was that we treated the dependency like a normal integration task instead of a release-critical blocker. Going forward, I want to flag those dependencies in the weekly review before they become timeline risk."

### Main move being trained

- bottom line before backstory
- direct ownership without panic

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 13: Pre-Handle Objections Before The Room Raises Them`

## Roleplay 2: Interviewer asks a question you did not prepare for

### Situation

The interviewer asks:

> "Tell me about a time you made the wrong call with incomplete information."

### Weak first answer

> "That's a good question. I mean, there are probably a few situations. I guess one that comes to mind is... actually let me think..."

### Stronger first answer

> "One example was a launch decision where I pushed speed too hard. The mistake was assuming early usage noise meant the product was healthy. It turned out the onboarding drop-off was masking the real issue."

### Escalation turn

The interviewer asks:

> "What should you have done differently in that moment?"

### Good second-turn shape

- one beat first
- lesson without self-punishment
- what decision rule changed

### Stronger second answer

> "I should have separated activity from successful activation. The better move would have been to slow down one week, check where new users were dropping, and make the launch decision with that data instead of momentum alone."

### Main move being trained

- first-sentence composure
- honest uncertainty without collapse

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 15: Rehearse The Pressure Shape, Not Just The Content`

## Roleplay 3: Stakeholder disagrees with your recommendation

### Situation

You recommend narrowing scope. The stakeholder says:

> "I think cutting this feature is the wrong signal to send."

### Weak first answer

> "I don't think that's really the issue. We already talked about this and I feel like if we keep everything in scope it's going to create even more problems."

### Stronger first answer

> "I see the concern. The part I read differently is the signal users care about most right now. I think reliability matters more than breadth in this release, which is why I'm recommending we protect the core flow first."

### Escalation turn

The stakeholder asks:

> "Are you saying the feature doesn't matter?"

### Good second-turn shape

- clarify the misunderstanding
- hold the position
- reframe around sequencing, not disrespect

### Stronger second answer

> "No, I think it does matter. I'm saying the order matters. If we ship a broader release that feels unstable, the feature gets less credit anyway. I'd rather land the reliable version first and add the rest on stronger ground."

### Main move being trained

- acknowledge before disagreeing
- hold position without sharpness

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 19: Take A Position, Give A Reason, Then Reopen`

## Roleplay 4: Social opener that needs to go deeper

### Situation

You meet someone at an event. They say:

> "Yeah, work has been busy lately."

### Weak first answer

> "Same. What do you do exactly?"

### Stronger first answer

> "Busy in a good way or in a draining way?"

### Escalation turn

They say:

> "A little of both. We just finished a launch that took over my life for two months."

### Good second-turn shape

- show interest in the real experience
- ask for texture
- do not switch back to yourself too quickly

### Stronger second answer

> "That sounds intense. What ended up being harder about it than people on the outside would guess?"

### Main move being trained

- visible curiosity
- stronger follow-up question for depth

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 8: Use Curiosity To Create Better Conversations`
- `Approach 16: Start With Connection Before Content`

## Roleplay 5: Humor line in a tense but ordinary meeting

### Situation

The meeting has drifted for twenty minutes around a tiny issue.

### Weak first answer

> "This is probably a dumb joke, but wow, this really could have been an email, haha."

### Stronger first answer

> "We have somehow built the rare three-minute decision that now has a full mythology."

### Escalation turn

Someone laughs and adds:

> "At this rate we'll need a sequel."

### Good second-turn shape

- build with the moment
- do not try to top everyone
- keep it short

### Stronger second answer

> "Director's cut coming next quarter."

### Main move being trained

- shared-reality humor
- collaborative play instead of solo performance

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 11: Use Humor As Shared Relief, Not Performance`
- `Approach 17: Put The Turn Late And Leave Room For The Laugh`

## Roleplay 6: Presentation opening followed by a skeptical question

### Situation

You open with:

> "Three weeks ago, a new customer got stuck before they ever reached the part of the product we were proudest of."

Then someone interrupts:

> "Isn't that just one anecdote?"

### Weak response

> "Well, yes, but I just meant it as an example and there are also other things behind it."

### Stronger response

> "It is one anecdote, but it points to the broader pattern. The reason I opened there is that the data and the lived experience are saying the same thing: setup friction is blocking activation."

### Escalation turn

They ask:

> "So what should we do differently?"

### Good second-turn shape

- move from anecdote to decision
- connect story to action

### Stronger second answer

> "We should simplify the first-run flow before adding more top-of-funnel traffic. More traffic into a leaky first experience just scales the wrong problem."

### Main move being trained

- story-first opening under challenge
- movement from scene to decision

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 4: Use The First Five Seconds To Establish Direction`
- `Approach 18: Build The Story Around One Embodied Moment`

## Roleplay 7: Explaining something complex to a non-expert

### Situation

Someone asks:

> "Can you explain what your team actually does without the technical version?"

### Weak first answer

> "Sure, we basically manage the orchestration layer for event-driven data processing across multiple internal services and..."

### Stronger first answer

> "We make sure customer actions turn into reliable product behavior instead of getting lost between systems."

### Escalation turn

They ask:

> "Can you give me a real example?"

### Good second-turn shape

- simple example
- no jargon rebound
- why it matters

### Stronger second answer

> "If someone changes a plan, we make sure billing, access, and reporting all update correctly. If that chain breaks, the customer feels it immediately even if they never see the systems behind it."

### Main move being trained

- complex-idea simplification
- audience-first explanation

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 14: Simplify Complex Ideas By Layering`

## Roleplay 8: Multilingual clarity under pressure

### Situation

You are explaining a delay in a second language and start feeling self-conscious.

The question is:

> "What is the main issue right now?"

### Weak first answer

> "The, um, there are several problems and one of them is maybe connected to the previous integration and we are still checking..."

### Stronger first answer

> "The main issue is the onboarding delay. New users are getting stuck before activation."

### Escalation turn

They ask:

> "What are you doing about it now?"

### Good second-turn shape

- one action
- one next step
- full phrase completion

### Stronger second answer

> "We are simplifying the first setup step today, and then we are checking where the drop is highest before changing anything else."

### Main move being trained

- clarity over accent anxiety
- structured pressure answer

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 14: Simplify Complex Ideas By Layering`
- `Approach 23: Improve Intelligibility Without Accent Erasure`
