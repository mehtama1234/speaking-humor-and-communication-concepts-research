# Speaking Pre-Flight Checklists

Last-minute preparation checklists for real speaking situations.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The atlas already helps learners:

- diagnose a weakness
- choose a pathway
- run a weekly sprint
- review stalled progress

What it still needed was a tighter real-world execution layer:

- what to do in the 5 to 20 minutes before an actual speaking moment

This file is for that last-mile gap.

It is not about deep practice design. It is about reducing obvious mistakes
before a real meeting, interview, presentation, conversation, or playful
social moment.

If you already know the event type but not the exact move to protect, start
with `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` first, then
use these checklists to keep that move alive right before the room.

## How to use this file

Use one checklist right before a real event.

Do not try to rehearse everything. The goal is to:

- clarify the point
- steady the first seconds
- avoid predictable failure patterns
- protect one exact transcript-backed move under real conditions

## Checklist 1: Before a meeting or work update

Ask:

1. What is the one thing this room needs from me?
2. What matters most here: update, recommendation, decision, or alignment?
3. Can I say the main point in one sentence?

Do:

- write one line for:
  - what is happening
  - why it matters
  - what next step is needed
- mark one line that deserves emphasis
- remove one apology, hedge, or throat-clearing opener

Avoid:

- starting with backstory
- reading your notes word-for-word
- ending without a clear next step

## Checklist 2: Before an interview or hard-question conversation

Ask:

1. What is the likely pressure question?
2. What would my clean first sentence be if I paused first?
3. What can I say if I do not know the full answer yet?

Do:

- rehearse one two-second pause before answering
- prepare one bottom-line-first answer
- prepare one calm deferral line for questions that need follow-up

Useful deferral pattern:

- `I want to answer that carefully, so here is the short version now and what I would look at next.`

Avoid:

- answering from panic speed
- dumping your whole thought process before your point
- treating uncertainty like failure

## Checklist 3: Before a presentation or formal talk

Ask:

1. What should the audience remember 10 minutes after I finish?
2. Is my opening line concrete enough to earn attention?
3. Which line deserves the biggest pause or emphasis?

Do:

- rehearse only the opening, transition, and close
- mark the first sentence where you can slow down
- choose one place to pause instead of accelerating

Avoid:

- over-memorizing every sentence
- beginning too abstractly
- spending all your prep on slides instead of delivery

## Checklist 4: Before a social conversation or networking event

Ask:

1. How will I show interest before trying to sound interesting?
2. What is one easy opener I can use naturally?
3. What follow-up question can move beyond surface talk?

Do:

- start from observation, appreciation, or curiosity
- choose one simple opener rather than waiting for the perfect one
- remind yourself that follow-up questions carry more weight than clever self-display

Avoid:

- trying to impress in the first 20 seconds
- forcing humor before warmth exists
- interrogating without bringing your own energy

## Checklist 5: Before a playful or humor-heavy interaction

Ask:

1. Am I trying to connect or trying to perform?
2. Can I build from what is already happening instead of forcing a line?
3. If a light joke lands, can I leave a beat instead of explaining it?

Do:

- look for something specific, shared, and emotionally colored
- keep the line short
- let the beat after the line do some of the work

Avoid:

- explaining your own joke
- switching topics just to deploy something prepared
- treating humor like a test of worth

## The universal 60-second reset

If you only have one minute before speaking:

1. Breathe out fully once.
2. Write the one point you need to land.
3. Decide how your first sentence should sound:
   - calm
   - direct
   - human
4. Choose one pause you will allow instead of rushing to fill silence.
5. Drop one thing you do not need to say.

## Fast failure prevention table

| Situation | Most common failure | Fastest correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meeting or update | too much context before the point | state the point and next step first |
| Interview or challenge | speed under pressure | pause before the first sentence |
| Presentation | abstract opening and flat delivery | strengthen the opening and mark one emphasis line |
| Social conversation | self-conscious performance | ask one real follow-up question |
| Humor moment | over-explaining the line | leave a beat and move on |
