# YouTube Channel Shortlist

First-pass channel shortlist for learning how to become a stronger speaker with
better delivery, clearer structure, stronger presence, and more deliberate use
of humor and storytelling.

Captured on August 9, 2026.

## Recommended core channels

### 1. Ultraspeaking

- link: `https://www.youtube.com/c/ultraspeaking`
- why it belongs:
  - strong fit for fear reduction, spontaneity, and speaking without scripts
  - good anchor for drills, flow, musicality, and speaking confidence
  - directly aligned with your example

### 2. Vinh Giang

- link: `https://www.youtube.com/@askvinh`
- why it belongs:
  - one of the strongest channels for delivery, vocal range, stage presence, and audience engagement
  - unusually good at making abstract speaking advice practical
  - strong crossover between communication, performance, and charisma

### 3. Think Fast Talk Smart

- link: `https://www.youtube.com/@ThinkFastTalkSmart`
- why it belongs:
  - excellent for structured communication, speaking under pressure, and reducing speaking anxiety
  - strong fit for interviews, meetings, leadership communication, and organized thought
  - useful complement to more performance-heavy channels

### 4. Charisma on Command

- link: `https://www.youtube.com/user/charismaoncommand`
- why it belongs:
  - useful for charisma, confidence, likability, humor, and social presence
  - good source for examples drawn from public figures
  - especially useful for conversational speaking rather than formal presentation alone

### 5. Toastmasters International

- link: `https://www.youtube.com/toastmasters`
- why it belongs:
  - strong source of speech examples, competition speeches, and classic public-speaking fundamentals
  - useful for openings, structure, audience engagement, and speech craft
  - valuable because it contains strong examples, not just advice

### 6. TED

- link: `https://www.youtube.com/ted`
- why it belongs:
  - high-value source of polished examples of speaking, storytelling, clarity, and persuasion
  - useful for studying speech architecture, pacing, emotional movement, and memorable phrasing

### 7. TEDx Talks

- link: `https://www.youtube.com/user/TEDxTalks`
- why it belongs:
  - broader and noisier than TED, but extremely valuable as an example library
  - useful for comparing strong and weak talks, hooks, story arcs, and audience energy management

### 8. Communication Coach Alexander Lyon

- link: `https://www.youtube.com/@alexanderlyon`
- why it belongs:
  - practical business communication and workplace speaking
  - useful for clarity, executive presence, meetings, conflict, and professional tone

## How to use this shortlist

The best build is probably a three-layer source stack:

1. `Ultraspeaking`, `Vinh Giang`, and `Think Fast Talk Smart` for drills and frameworks.
2. `Toastmasters`, `TED`, and `TEDx Talks` for examples of actual speeches.
3. `Charisma on Command` and `Alexander Lyon` for conversational presence, persuasion, and workplace communication.

## Early concept buckets likely to emerge

- speaking without a script
- managing speaking anxiety
- creating flow while thinking live
- hooks and openings
- vocal variety
- stage presence
- conversational charisma
- humor through surprise and specificity
- storytelling as structure rather than ornament
- speaking drills that create real improvement
