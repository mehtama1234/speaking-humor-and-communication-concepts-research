# Business Communication Curriculum

Focused self-training sequence for stronger updates, recommendations, pitches,
and stakeholder communication.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The general starter curriculum is useful, but some learners do not need a
balanced speaking overview first.

They need a more direct answer to:

- How do I sound clearer, more decisive, and more useful at work?

This file turns the current business-facing layers of the atlas into a compact
ordered sequence for founder-style, workplace, and client-facing
communication.

- how stronger speakers get to the point earlier
- how they separate answer from background
- how they sound firm without sounding rigid
- how they turn updates into direction and next step

## Who this curriculum is for

Best for learners who:

- sound less strategic than they actually are
- bury the point under backstory or explanation
- need stronger updates, recommendations, stakeholder asks, or pitches
- want to sound more decisive without becoming stiff or aggressive

## Six-step business communication sequence

### Step 1: Clarify what the communication is trying to do

Study:

- `concepts/audience-first-communication.md`
- `concepts/founder-style-and-business-communication.md`

Watch:

- `Know Before You Tell - The Three-Part Framework Every Communicator Needs`

Practice:

- `goal-audience-data planning card`

Exact approach to anchor:

- `Approach 5: Prepare with goal, audience, and constraints`

Goal:

- stop treating updates and pitches like information release

## Step 2: Make recommendations easier to act on

Study:

- `concepts/structure-as-leverage.md`
- `concepts/persuasion-and-influence.md`

Watch:

- `How to Pitch Anything: 10 20 30 Rule by Guy Kawasaki`

Practice:

- `PREP argument reps`

Exact approach to anchor:

- `Approach 6: Compress before you expand`
- `Approach 22: Aim for the next step, not immediate conversion`

Goal:

- move from long explanation toward point, reason, and next step

Concrete model:

- study Guy Kawasaki in `analysis/real-speaker-exemplars.md`
- study `Example 2` in `analysis/workplace-speaking-deployment-examples.md`
- study `Example 3` in
  `analysis/real-world-speaking-application-examples.md`
- then rewrite the `project update in a meeting` example from
  `analysis/worked-speaking-situation-examples.md` using his compression
  standard

## Step 3: Remove visible weakness from business language

Study:

- `concepts/executive-presence.md`
- `concepts/founder-style-and-business-communication.md`

Watch:

- `How To Stop Sounding Unprofessional At Work`

Practice:

- rewrite one recommendation without softeners
- answer the actual question before adding context

Exact approach to anchor:

- `Approach 7: Remove low-status leakage`

Goal:

- sound cleaner, firmer, and more believable in professional settings

Concrete model:

- use `Example 3` from
  `analysis/workplace-speaking-deployment-examples.md`
- use `Example 1` or `Example 5` from
  `analysis/real-world-speaking-application-examples.md`
- use the `disagreeing with a stakeholder` example in
  `analysis/worked-speaking-situation-examples.md`
- compare the weak version against the stronger version and remove
  apology-first wording from one real message you need to deliver this week

## Step 4: Make routine updates feel directional instead of flat

Study:

- `concepts/musicality.md`
- `concepts/founder-style-and-business-communication.md`

Watch:

- `How to Speak like a CEO`

Practice:

- `executive update with felt emphasis`

Exact approach to anchor:

- `Approach 21: Use unhurried pace to claim space`

Goal:

- communicate what matters, not just what occurred

## Step 5: Handle pushback without losing shape

Study:

- `concepts/hard-question-handling.md`
- `concepts/pause-as-strength.md`

Watch:

- `How to Answer ANY Question (Even If You Don't Know The Answer!)`
- `Ask Matt Anything: Authenticity, Anxiety, and Answering Well`

Practice:

- `pressure-question five-second reset`

Exact approach to anchor:

- `Approach 3: Pause, frame, then answer`
- `Approach 19: Take a position, give a reason, then reopen`

Goal:

- stay useful when certainty is incomplete or the room becomes more adversarial

Concrete model:

- study Sam Altman and Matt Abrahams in
  `analysis/real-speaker-exemplars.md`
- review `Example 4` in
  `analysis/workplace-speaking-deployment-examples.md`
- review `Example 1` and `Example 5` in
  `analysis/real-world-speaking-application-examples.md`
- then practice the `answering a hard question` example in
  `analysis/worked-speaking-situation-examples.md`
- copy the sequence:
  - pause
  - answer the concern
  - name what is known
  - name what happens next

## Step 6: Study the weak-versus-strong business contrasts

Study:

- `analysis/contrast-cases.md`
  Focus on:
  - apology-wrapped recommendation versus clean business recommendation
  - backstory overload versus answer-the-question-first communication
  - flat information dump versus decision-oriented business update

Review:

- `analysis/evidence-moments.md`
  Focus on:
  - softeners make business recommendations sound weaker
  - answer the question before the backstory
  - the goal of a pitch is the next step, not immediate conversion

Goal:

- become able to hear the difference between weak business speech and stronger
  business speech

## Suggested two-week rhythm

### Week 1

1. Steps 1 to 3
2. Focus on structure, recommendation quality, and professional credibility

### Week 2

1. Steps 4 to 6
2. Focus on delivery, pushback handling, and contrast recognition

## Minimum viable business practice rule

If the learner only adopts one rule from this curriculum:

- every update, pitch, or recommendation must answer:
  - what is happening
  - why it matters
  - what next step is needed
