## Drill 1: Accordion speaking

Purpose:
Train speaking from internalized meaning instead of memorized wording.

Likely source sessions:

- `#10 How to Speak like a CEO`
- `#4 How to Speak Brilliantly Well – Without a Script (Ali Abdaal)`

How to run it:

1. Pick one idea you can explain in five minutes.
2. Deliver it in five minutes.
3. Compress it to two and a half minutes.
4. Compress it to one minute.
5. Compress it to thirty seconds.
6. Expand back upward while keeping the same core message.

What it appears to build:

- message internalization
- spontaneity
- concise thinking
- non-scripted fluency

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 1: Talking-Points Navigation Instead Of Script Recital`
- `Approach 6: Compress Before You Expand`

## Drill 2: Stay-in-character pause recovery

Purpose:
Train visible composure when you lose your place.

Likely source sessions:

- `#19 How to be perceived as more confident`
- `#12 How Tristan de Montebello got to the World Championships of Public Speaking`

How to run it:

1. Speak on a topic for two to three minutes.
2. Have a partner interrupt you at random or force a blank by asking you to stop.
3. Do not apologize immediately.
4. Pause, breathe, keep posture, and resume only after finding your next line of thought.

What it appears to build:

- composure under disruption
- pause tolerance
- recovery without visible panic

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 15: Rehearse The Pressure Shape, Not Just The Content`

## Drill 3: Musicality contrast reps

Purpose:
Train vocal range and expressive variation.

Likely source sessions:

- `#18 How to get people to listen to you (musicality lecture)`
- `#20 How to sound smarter (TEDx breakdown)`

How to run it:

1. Record yourself saying the same short passage in a flat monotone.
2. Record it again with wider pitch, volume, pacing, and pause variation.
3. Compare which version feels more alive and easier to follow.
4. Repeat with your own work or meeting updates.

What it appears to build:

- vocal variety
- listener engagement
- awareness of expressive range

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 10: Sound More Alive Through Musicality`
- `Approach 21: Use Unhurried Pace To Claim Space`

## Drill 4: Rapid-fire analogies

Purpose:
Separate confidence from content certainty and sharpen on-the-fly verbal generation.

Likely source sessions:

- `#15 Train Your Brain to SPEAK BETTER! (3 online games)`

How to run it:

1. Generate random nouns or have a partner provide them.
2. Complete prompts like “A banana is like scissors because...”
3. Keep moving even when the content is weak.
4. Prioritize saying something over saying something perfect.

What it appears to build:

- fast association
- spontaneous expression
- confidence under uncertainty

## Drill 5: Triple-step focus speaking

Purpose:
Train staying on one clear thread while integrating distractions.

Likely source sessions:

- `#15 Train Your Brain to SPEAK BETTER! (3 online games)`

How to run it:

1. Speak on one topic.
2. Inject random words that must be incorporated without losing the main thread.
3. Keep the through-line coherent while absorbing interruptions.

What it appears to build:

- focus
- idea continuity
- on-the-fly integration

## Drill 6: Executive update with felt emphasis

Purpose:
Practice making dry information sound alive by deciding what matters emotionally.

Likely source sessions:

- `#10 How to Speak like a CEO`

How to run it:

1. Take a routine project update.
2. Ask, “What matters here?” and “How do I want the audience to feel?”
3. Re-deliver the update with those priorities emphasized.

What it appears to build:

- audience engagement
- energetic emphasis
- relevance filtering

## Drill 7: Small-talk entry reps

Purpose:
Make low-stakes social openings more natural for shy or overthinking speakers.

Likely source sessions:

- `#9 3 Small Talk Tips for Introverts (That Actually Work!)`

How to run it:

1. Prepare several low-pressure opening lines or questions.
2. Use them in brief real interactions or role-play reps.
3. Focus on ease of entry rather than cleverness.

What it appears to build:

- conversational initiation
- social comfort
- audience connection

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 8: Use Curiosity To Create Better Conversations`
- `Approach 16: Start With Connection Before Content`
