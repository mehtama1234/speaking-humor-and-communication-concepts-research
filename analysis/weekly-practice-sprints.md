# Weekly Practice Sprints

Seven-day execution plans for turning the speaking research atlas into actual
repetition.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The repo already has:

- concepts
- drills
- learner pathways
- focused improvement actions
- starter curricula

What it still needed was a tighter execution layer:

- what to do across the next seven days if your problem is specific and urgent

These sprints are not long-term programs. They are short, concrete training
blocks that make the atlas easier to use in real life.

If you know the weekly bottleneck but not the exact move to train, start with
`analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` first, then use
the sprint as the repetition structure around that move.

## How to use these sprints

- Pick one sprint only.
- Pick one exact approach from
  `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`.
- Run it for seven days before mixing in another.
- Keep each daily rep short enough that you actually do it.
- Review at the end of day seven:
  - what got easier
  - what still collapses
  - which pathway or concept should be studied next

If you want these same seven-day blocks organized by learner type instead of by
skill sprint, also use:

- `analysis/weekly-plans-by-learner-type.md`

If you want one live transfer assignment to anchor the week, also use:

- `analysis/live-field-missions.md`

If you want the real person, the matching room, and a stronger borrowed version
before the daily rep, also use:

- `analysis/real-world-speaking-application-examples.md`

## Sprint 1: Stop freezing and start answering more calmly

Best for:

- learners who blank when attention lands on them
- speakers who panic in the first seconds of an answer
- people who rush because silence feels dangerous

Day 1:

- Study `concepts/pause-as-strength.md`
- Run `pressure-question five-second reset` three times

Day 2:

- Study `concepts/speaking-under-pressure.md`
- Record three 30-second answers where you start with a pause instead of speed

Day 3:

- Run `stay-in-character pause recovery`
- Practice one deliberate recovery after stopping mid-thought

Day 4:

- Watch `The First 5 Seconds: How to Start a Speech the Moment All Eyes Suddenly Turn to You | AMA`
- Repeat only the opening 10 to 20 seconds five times

Day 5:

- Answer three harder questions with a bottom-line-first opening
- Review where you still leaked apology or panic

Day 6:

- Use one real pause before answering in a meeting or conversation
- Write down how the pause changed the feeling of control

Day 7:

- Re-record day 1 and day 2 answers
- Compare:
  - pace
  - pause tolerance
  - visible composure

## Sprint 2: Stop rambling and make your answers easier to follow

Best for:

- learners who know the idea but explain it badly
- speakers whose answers feel long but still unclear
- professionals whose updates lack shape

Day 1:

- Study `concepts/structure-as-leverage.md`
- Use `goal-audience-data card` on one real communication task

Day 2:

- Run `PREP argument reps` on three common questions
- Keep each answer under 45 seconds

Day 3:

- Study `concepts/audience-first-communication.md`
- Rewrite one explanation for a less-informed listener

Day 4:

- Watch `Know Before You Tell - The Three-Part Framework Every Communicator Needs`
- Distill one upcoming explanation into:
  - goal
  - audience
  - one point

Day 5:

- Record one work update with:
  - what is happening
  - why it matters
  - what comes next

Day 6:

- In one real conversation, answer the question first before any backstory
- Review whether the listener got the point faster

Day 7:

- Re-answer the same three questions from day 2
- Compare:
  - length
  - clarity
  - whether the point arrives earlier

## Sprint 3: Sound more credible and less weak at work

Best for:

- professionals who sound softer than they think
- speakers who clutter updates with fillers and apology language
- learners trying to improve executive presence quickly

Day 1:

- Study `concepts/executive-presence.md`
- Record one 60-second work update

Day 2:

- Study `concepts/articulation-and-verbal-polish.md`
- Remove filler words, trailing endings, and apology openers from yesterday’s update

Day 3:

- Run `pause replacement reps`
- Deliver the same update more slowly with one clear pause before the main point

Day 4:

- Watch `How To Stop Sounding Unprofessional At Work`
- Write three weak work phrases you use and rewrite them more directly

Day 5:

- Give one recommendation without:
  - `sorry`
  - `maybe`
  - `this might be wrong`

Day 6:

- Record one decision-oriented update:
  - what happened
  - why it matters
  - what needs to happen next

Day 7:

- Ask one trusted colleague or friend:
  - what single speech habit most weakens my credibility?
- Fold that note into next week’s practice

## Sprint 4: Become warmer and less awkward in conversation

Best for:

- learners who hesitate entering conversation
- speakers who try too hard to impress
- people who want connection to feel more natural

Day 1:

- Study `concepts/curiosity-and-conversational-depth.md`
- Use one appreciation-first opener in a live conversation

Day 2:

- Run `small-talk entry reps`
- Practice three low-pressure openers out loud

Day 3:

- Study `concepts/self-respect-over-approval-seeking.md`
- In one interaction, aim to sound interested instead of impressive

Day 4:

- Watch `Hello, Stranger: Why Curiosity Beats Charisma Every Time`
- Ask two follow-up questions before telling your own long story

Day 5:

- Run `small-talk to depth ladder`
- Move one conversation one layer deeper with a better follow-up

Day 6:

- Use `positive-intent rewrite` after one awkward or ambiguous moment
- Write the generous interpretation and the calmer next move

Day 7:

- Review the week:
  - where did you feel less self-conscious?
  - where did curiosity reduce pressure?
  - which moments still triggered impress mode?

## Sprint 5: Make humor feel more natural and less forced

Best for:

- learners who want more warmth through humor
- speakers whose funny ideas arrive too late
- people whose humor feels rushed or too performative

Day 1:

- Study `concepts/humor-as-connection.md`
- Retell one minor frustration with more relatability and less punchline chasing

Day 2:

- Study `concepts/humor-timing-and-collaborative-play.md`
- Move the funniest or strangest word to the end of three sentences

Day 3:

- Watch `The Science of Funny: How Humor Builds Connection`
- Notice:
  - setup
  - emotional color
  - pause after the turn

Day 4:

- In one conversation, build one playful line from what the other person just
  said instead of launching a fresh joke

Day 5:

- After one playful line, leave one extra beat of silence instead of rescuing
  it with explanation

Day 6:

- Review one missed humor moment and write:
  - the setup
  - the turn
  - what made the timing late

Day 7:

- Re-tell the same short story from day 1 with better placement and pause
- Compare whether it feels more human, lighter, and easier to join

## What this adds

These sprints make the repo more course-like in a different way than the
starter curriculum does.

The starter curriculum answers:

- what sequence should I study first?

These practice sprints answer:

- what exactly should I do for the next seven days if I want one problem to get
  better?
