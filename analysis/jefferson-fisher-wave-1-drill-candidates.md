## 1. Defensive Rewrite Drill

Use when:

- you notice yourself explaining, justifying, or protecting too quickly

Drill:

1. Write three recent moments where you got defensive.
2. Write your actual first response.
3. Rewrite it in a shorter, steadier form.
4. Practice delivering the shorter version without speed-up.

Likely supporting sessions:

- `#27 What To Say Instead Of Getting Defensive`
- `#54 How to Be Direct Without Sounding Rude`

## 2. Three-Second Pause Drill

Use when:

- emotional rooms make you react too quickly

Drill:

1. Have a partner read a provoking line.
2. Wait three seconds before responding.
3. Respond in one sentence only.
4. Repeat until the pause feels natural instead of weak.

Likely supporting sessions:

- `#108 The 3 Second Rule That Ends HIGHLY Emotional Argument`
- `#22 How to Stay in Control During Heated Debates`

## 3. Interruption Recovery Drill

Use when:

- people talk over you and you either shrink or snap

Drill:

1. Practice one interruption-recovery phrase out loud.
2. Have a partner interrupt you mid-sentence three times.
3. Reclaim the floor using the same calm phrase each time.
4. Focus on tone and pacing more than wording variety.

Likely supporting sessions:

- `#16 The One Phrase That Instantly Stops Someone Talking Over You`
- `#62 How To Stop Someone From Talking Over You (3 Simple Steps)`

## 4. Calm Voice Drill

Use when:

- your voice climbs or tightens during pressure

Drill:

1. Record a one-minute response to a hostile prompt.
2. Re-record it at a slower pace and lower vocal placement.
3. Compare which version sounds more trustworthy and in control.

Likely supporting sessions:

- `#42 Why Lowering Your Voice Makes You Unstoppable`
- `#34 How to Stay Calm, Confident, and In Control...`

## 5. Disagreement Without Winning Drill

Use when:

- you treat disagreement like a verdict instead of a conversation

Drill:

1. Pick one view you strongly disagree with.
2. Practice responding without trying to defeat it in the first sentence.
3. Use a response that separates understanding from agreement.
4. End with your position stated cleanly and briefly.

Likely supporting sessions:

- `#81 You don't need to win or attend every argument you're invited to`
- `#103 How To Talk To Someone You Completely Disagree With`
