# Communication Coach Alexander Lyon Wave 1 Drill Candidates

First-pass drill candidates extracted from the `Communication Coach Alexander
Lyon` starter corpus.

Date: August 10, 2026.

If these drill candidates are being used to shape learner practice, pair them
with `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`.

## Best candidate drills

### 1. Plain-language rewrite drill

Transcript-backed basis:

- `#78 How to Communicate Clearly and Concisely`
- `#353 Concise Sentences`

Run it like this:

1. Take one paragraph from a recent email, update, or explanation.
2. Replace every fancy, inflated, or vague word with the plain-English version.
3. Read both versions aloud.
4. Keep the one that sounds easier to process and stronger to say.

Why it matters:

- directly attacks camouflage language
- builds workplace clarity through repetition

### 2. Context-intent frame drill

Transcript-backed basis:

- `#84 How to Get to the Point when Speaking`
- `#54 How to Communicate Like a CEO`

Run it like this:

1. Before starting a conversation, say the topic in one short line.
2. Then say why the listener is hearing it.
3. Only after those two lines should you add the detail.
4. Practice this on three real updates in one day.

Why it matters:

- converts rambling into a framing habit
- directly builds `Approach 26`

### 3. Honest-uncertainty response drill

Transcript-backed basis:

- `#67 How To Say "I don't know" With Credibility!`

Run it like this:

1. Write three realistic questions you might get asked that you cannot answer
   perfectly on the spot.
2. Practice three response patterns:
   `I don't know, but I'll find out.`
   `I don't know, but X would know.`
   `I don't know exactly, but here's what I do know.`
3. Record yourself saying each one calmly.

Why it matters:

- reduces panic under scrutiny
- strengthens `Approach 3` and `Approach 13`

### 4. Message-compression drill

Transcript-backed basis:

- `#354 Concise Message`
- `#355 Long-windedness`

Run it like this:

1. Take one answer you normally give in two minutes.
2. Cut it to 30 seconds.
3. Then cut it again to 15 seconds.
4. Keep the shortest version that still makes sense.

Why it matters:

- builds a repeatable anti-rambling habit
- sharpens `Approach 6`

### 5. Pause-and-conviction drill

Transcript-backed basis:

- `#79 How to Speak with Conviction`
- `#351 Effective Pauses`

Run it like this:

1. Record a one-minute recommendation or opinion.
2. Re-record it with deliberate pauses before key points and at sentence turns.
3. Compare whether the second version sounds calmer and more credible.

Why it matters:

- converts pause from hesitation into authority
- supports conviction and executive presence

### 6. Meeting-preparation drill

Transcript-backed basis:

- `#113 How to Run an Effective Meeting 5 Tips`

Run it like this:

1. Before the next meeting, write the leader, agenda, and expected
   contribution for each participant.
2. Send those expectations in advance.
3. After the meeting, note whether participation improved.

Why it matters:

- turns vague meetings into designed meetings
- gives a visible workplace transfer from the corpus

### 7. Reflective-listening drill for professionals

Transcript-backed basis:

- `#118 Reflective Listening Skills`
- `#91 Effective Listening Skills for Leaders`

Run it like this:

1. In one work conversation, resist offering advice for the first two minutes.
2. Instead reflect the person’s feeling, thought, or dilemma back to them.
3. Keep your reflection concise and let them elaborate.

Why it matters:

- strengthens trust and clarity
- deepens the professional side of `Approach 25`

### 8. New-to-the-room contribution drill

Transcript-backed basis:

- `#348 Effective Communication Skills When You're New to a Group or Team`
- `#113 How to Run an Effective Meeting 5 Tips`

Run it like this:

1. In a group where you have low familiarity, contribute in three ways:
   ask one question, offer one point of view, and build on one other person’s
   idea.
2. Do this without waiting for perfect confidence.

Why it matters:

- helps newer or lower-status contributors become visible
- creates transfer into meetings and teams quickly
