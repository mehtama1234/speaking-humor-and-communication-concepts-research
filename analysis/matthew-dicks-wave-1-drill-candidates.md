# Matthew Dicks Wave 1 Drill Candidates

First-pass drill candidates suggested by the `Matthew Dicks` starter corpus.

Date: August 10, 2026.

If these drills are being used to drive learner-facing extraction, pair them

## 1. Story Worthiness Filter

Use when:

- you know you should tell more stories, but your stories feel flat

Drill:

1. Write down five recent moments from your week.
2. For each one, ask:
   did something change, surprise, reveal, or collide?
3. Keep only the moments that create movement.
4. Try telling only one of those out loud in under two minutes.

Likely supporting sessions:

- `#9 The Story Problem Nobody Talks About`
- `#25 How to Know If a Story Is Worth Telling`

## 2. First-30-Seconds Rewrite

Use when:

- your openings sound like explanation, summary, or throat-clearing

Drill:

1. Take an existing story opening.
2. Remove the teaser sentence and the explanation sentence.
3. Restart from a live moment, spoken line, or concrete action.
4. Compare which version pulls attention faster.

Likely supporting sessions:

- `#4 Stop Using Story Hooks. Do This in the First 30s.`
- `#48 Why Attention Is Earned Through Story`

## 3. Scene Before Lesson

Use when:

- you rush to the message before the audience cares

Drill:

1. Tell a story once in your normal style.
2. Retell it with one rule:
   no lesson statement until the listener can picture the moment.
3. Notice how long you can delay the conclusion while still keeping attention.

Likely supporting sessions:

- `#13 The Storytelling Choice That Made This Ad Work`
- `#46 Why Speeches Are Forgotten Without Story`

## 4. Exemplar Reverse Engineering

Use when:

- you need to learn from strong performances instead of only abstract advice

Drill:

1. Read or listen to one exemplar story.
2. Mark where tension increases, where the turn happens, and where the payoff
   lands.
3. Summarize the story architecture in five beats.
4. Apply the same beat pattern to one of your own stories.

Likely supporting sessions:

- `#11 Charity Thief`
- `#31 The Robbery`
- `#50 The Story That Won The Moth GrandSLAM`

## 5. Attention Test

Use when:

- you are unsure why a story feels dull

Drill:

1. Read your story aloud.
2. Mark every place where the story pauses to explain instead of move.
3. Replace one explanation beat with a more specific moment or action.
4. Retell it and compare the attention level.

Likely supporting sessions:

- `#47 The Mistake That Breaks a Story`
- `#48 Why Attention Is Earned Through Story`
