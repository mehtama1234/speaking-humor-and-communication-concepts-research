Concrete before/after speaking situations built from the repo's transcript-backed

## Purpose

Several files in this repo already cover concepts, drills, and practice routes.
This file adds a denser transfer layer:

- what a weak version sounds like in a real situation
- what a stronger version sounds like instead

These are not meant to be memorized line-for-line. They are shape examples.

If you already know the room but not the exact move name, open

## Situation 1: Giving a project update in a meeting

### Common weak version

> "So, um, I just wanted to give a quick update. We looked at a few options last
> week, and there were some issues with the data pipeline, and then design had a
> couple questions, and engineering is still working through some dependencies.
> So yeah, we're still kind of figuring it out."

### Stronger version

> "Quick update: we're on track for the Friday review, but the main risk is the
> data pipeline. I'm recommending we cut the low-priority dashboard piece, ship
> the core workflow first, and resolve the pipeline issue in parallel."

### What changed

- the point arrived first
- the risk was named clearly
- the recommendation was audible
- the next step became visible

### Transcript-backed mechanism

- `How to Stop Sounding Unprofessional at Work`
- `Know Before You Tell - The Three-Part Framework Every Communicator Needs`
- `Evidence: answer-the-question-before-the-backstory`

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 5: Prepare With Goal, Audience, And Constraints`
- `Approach 13: Pre-Handle Objections Before The Room Raises Them`

### How to use it live

Before your next update, write these three lines:

- status
- main risk
- recommendation or next step

If you feel yourself drifting into context sprawl, stop and restart with:

> "The short version is..."

## Situation 2: Answering a hard question when you do not know everything yet

### Common weak version

> "Yeah, that's a good question. I mean, I think there are a lot of factors. We
> have not fully finished looking at all the options yet, and there are probably
> different ways to think about it, so I do not want to say the wrong thing..."

### Stronger version

> "The honest answer is that we do not know the full number yet. What we do know
> is that churn increased after the onboarding change, and the next check I would
> run is whether the drop is concentrated in first-week users or spread across
> the whole funnel."

### What changed

- uncertainty was owned instead of hidden
- one grounded fact replaced vague hand-waving
- the answer kept moving instead of collapsing
- credibility came from clarity, not fake certainty

### Transcript-backed mechanism

- `How to Answer ANY Question (Even If You Don't Know The Answer!)`
- `How Sam Altman Answers Impossible Questions`
- `Evidence: composed-pauses-improve-hard-question-answers`

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 13: Pre-Handle Objections Before The Room Raises Them`

### How to use it live

Use this answer shape:

- what we know
- what we do not know yet
- what I would check next

A usable sentence starter is:

> "What we know so far is..."

## Situation 3: Disagreeing with a stakeholder without sounding defensive

### Common weak version

> "I do not think that is really the issue. We already looked at that, and I
> feel like if we change direction now it is going to create even more problems."

### Stronger version

> "I see the concern. The part I read differently is the cause. I do think we
> have a real adoption problem, but I think it is coming from setup friction
> more than feature depth. If useful, I can show the two signals that make me
> think that."

### What changed

- acknowledgment came before rebuttal
- the disagreement became specific instead of emotional
- the speaker sounded collaborative, not cornered
- evidence was invited without forcing a fight

### Transcript-backed mechanism

- `Stanford's Top Negotiator Reveals What Most People Get Dead Wrong`
- `How to Disagree Without Starting an Argument`
- `Evidence: relationship-management-beats-win-the-argument-negotiation`
- `Evidence: position-plus-openness-beats-neutrality-in-disagreement`

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 19: Take A Position, Give A Reason, Then Reopen`

### How to use it live

Try this sequence:

- acknowledge the concern
- isolate the exact point of disagreement
- offer one reason or data point
- invite inspection instead of pushing harder

Use language like:

> "I see the concern. The part I read differently is..."

## Situation 4: Recovering after a conversational awkward moment

### Common weak version

> "Oh, sorry, that came out wrong. I did not mean it like that. Never mind."

### Stronger version

> "That landed clumsily. What I meant was that I was impressed you handled that
> calmly, not that it looked easy."

### What changed

- the speaker did not spiral
- the repair was direct and brief
- intent was clarified without a long self-defense speech
- the conversation was stabilized fast

### Transcript-backed mechanism

- `What To Say When A Conversation Gets Awkward`
- `Hello, Stranger: Why Curiosity Beats Charisma Every Time`
- `Evidence: positive-intent-and-direct-ownership-shrink-awkwardness`

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 9: Repair Awkwardness By Naming And Redirecting`

### How to use it live

When you misfire:

- name the misfire simply
- restate the intended meaning
- return to the conversation

Useful line:

> "That came out wrong. What I meant was..."

## Situation 5: Being warmer in small talk without trying to be impressive

### Common weak version

> "Yeah, work has been busy. Same old stuff. What about you?"

### Stronger version

> "Busy, but in an interesting way. We are trying to fix one part of the product
> that everyone touches and nobody notices unless it breaks. What kind of work
> has been taking most of your energy lately?"

### What changed

- the answer gave the other person something to grab onto
- the tone was human, not performative
- curiosity created momentum
- the follow-up question had more texture than generic small talk

### Transcript-backed mechanism

- `Hello, Stranger: Why Curiosity Beats Charisma Every Time`
- `3 Small Talk Tips for Introverts (That Actually Work!)`
- `Evidence: visible-appreciation-opens-conversation`

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 8: Use Curiosity To Create Better Conversations`
- `Approach 16: Start With Connection Before Content`

### How to use it live

Replace flat answers with:

- a short human detail
- a question that invites a real answer

Simple pattern:

> "Busy, but because of something interesting..."

## Situation 6: Making a light humorous line land

### Common weak version

> "I always make bad jokes. Anyway, I guess this meeting could have been an
> email, haha."

### Stronger version

> "We have now built the rare meeting that started as three bullets and somehow
> grew a mythology."

### What changed

- the humor came from shared reality
- the line had a clearer image
- there was no apology before the joke
- the speaker did not explain the line after saying it

### Transcript-backed mechanism

- `The Science of Funny: How Humor Builds Connection`
- `How To Instantly Make Any Conversation More Fun`
- `Evidence: humor-lands-better-when-setup-and-emotional-color-arrive-before-the-turn`
- `Evidence: connection-oriented-humor-is-co-created-not-performed`

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 11: Use Humor As Shared Relief, Not Performance`
- `Approach 17: Put The Turn Late And Leave Room For The Laugh`

### How to use it live

Do not begin with "this is a bad joke, but..."

Instead:

- notice a shared absurdity
- describe it with a clean image
- leave one beat after the line

## Situation 7: Starting a presentation or answer with more presence

### Common weak version

> "Hi everyone. Thanks for being here. Today I am going to talk a little bit
> about our onboarding strategy and some of the things we learned..."

### Stronger version

> "Three weeks ago, a new customer got stuck in our setup flow before they ever
> saw the product work. That one moment explains why today's topic matters."

### What changed

- attention was captured early
- the audience entered a moment, not a topic heading
- relevance appeared before detail
- the opening sounded deliberate rather than procedural

### Transcript-backed mechanism

- `The First 5 Seconds: How to Start a Speech the Moment All Eyes Suddenly Turn to You`
- `How To Make A Strong First Impression (That Lasts)`
- `Evidence: story-first-openings-build-connection-before-content`

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 4: Use The First Five Seconds To Establish Direction`
- `Approach 18: Build The Story Around One Embodied Moment`

### How to use it live

Before a talk, prepare one opening that does one of these:

- shows a vivid moment
- states an unexpected tension
- names the cost of not paying attention

Then stop. Do not stack three openings on top of each other.

## Situation 8: Telling a story that people can actually picture

### Common weak version

> "It taught me that leadership is really important and communication matters a
> lot when things get difficult."

### Stronger version

> "At 11:40 p.m. we were still in the office, the launch doc was open on three
> screens, and nobody wanted to say out loud that the rollback was probably the
> right move. That was the moment I learned what calm leadership actually looks
> like."

### What changed

- the listener could enter the scene
- the lesson came after the lived moment
- abstraction was replaced by concrete detail
- memorability increased immediately

### Transcript-backed mechanism

- `Transform Your Storytelling by Understanding These Three Layers`
- `Don't Lose the Room: How to Make Ideas Stick`
- `Evidence: specific-embodied-moments-beat-abstract-storytelling`

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 18: Build The Story Around One Embodied Moment`

### How to use it live

When telling a story, force yourself to include:

- one time marker
- one physical detail
- one tension point

Only then say what it meant.

## Situation 9: Speaking clearly under accent or second-language pressure

### Common weak version

> "Sorry for my accent. I know I speak too fast sometimes. What I am trying to
> say is maybe this feature is, um, not exactly ideal for all users..."

### Stronger version

> "The main issue is that first-time users are not seeing value quickly enough.
> I will say the recommendation first, then the two reasons behind it."

### What changed

- apology language disappeared
- clarity replaced self-consciousness
- structure reduced processing load for the listener
- the speaker optimized for intelligibility, not perfect sounding English

### Transcript-backed mechanism

- `Accent-uate, Don't Compensate: Why Clarity Matters More Than Perfection`
- `Stop Fixing Your Accent: What Linguists Know That Language Teachers Don't`
- `Evidence: intelligibility-can-improve-without-accent-erasure`

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 14: Simplify Complex Ideas By Layering`
- `Approach 23: Improve Intelligibility Without Accent Erasure`

### How to use it live

Before high-pressure speaking:

- choose the main point
- slow the first sentence slightly
- make your structure explicit

Useful phrase:

> "I will give the recommendation first, then the reasons."

## Situation 10: Asking a better follow-up so conversation gets deeper

### Common weak version

> "Oh nice. That sounds cool."

### Stronger version

> "What was the part of it that was actually hard once you were in it?"

### What changed

- the conversation moved from polite to real
- curiosity did the work instead of performance
- the other person got permission to say something non-obvious

### Transcript-backed mechanism

- `How to Have Deeper Conversations with Anyone`
- `Hello, Stranger: Why Curiosity Beats Charisma Every Time`
- `Evidence: visible-appreciation-opens-conversation`

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 8: Use Curiosity To Create Better Conversations`
- `Approach 16: Start With Connection Before Content`

### How to use it live

Prepare three follow-up question shapes:

- "What was harder about that than it looked?"
- "What surprised you once you were inside it?"
- "What changed your mind about it?"
