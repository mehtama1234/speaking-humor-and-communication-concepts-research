# Project Objective

Build a research workspace that turns speaking, humor, and communication
teaching material into a reusable concept system.

This project should answer questions like:

- What actually makes someone easier to listen to?
- What are the teachable components of a strong speaker?
- How do confident speakers structure thought under pressure?
- How do humor, timing, tension, contrast, and storytelling improve delivery?
- Which drills reliably improve fluency, presence, and audience connection?
- What examples best show good speaking versus weak speaking?

## What we want to extract

- concepts
- themes
- subthemes
- drills and exercises
- speaking frameworks
- audience-handling patterns
- humor mechanisms
- storytelling patterns
- delivery techniques
- example clips and transcripts

## Core concept families to expect

- confidence versus presence
- flow versus memorization
- vocal variety and pacing
- body language and eye contact
- audience attention and hooks
- story structure
- humor through surprise, contrast, escalation, and specificity
- thinking on your feet
- persuasion and clarity
- authenticity versus performance

## Output direction

Over time this workspace should become:

- a source map of the best channels and talks
- a transcript-backed concept atlas
- a practical drill library
- a pattern library for better speeches and better everyday speaking
- a reader-facing course or site on becoming a better speaker
