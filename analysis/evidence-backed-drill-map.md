# Evidence-Backed Drill Map

Transcript-backed explanation of why the current core drills exist and what
behavior each one is meant to change.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The atlas already has:

- drill candidates
- practice templates
- action guides
- evidence moments

What it still needed was a direct bridge between evidence and drill choice:

- why this drill
- which transcript-backed claim supports it
- what visible behavior it is trying to move

This file answers that.

If you want the named move before choosing the drill, start with
`analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` first. This file
works best as the evidence-to-drill layer built around those exact moves.

## How to use this file

Use this when:

- you want to understand why a drill matters before committing to it
- you have several drill options and need the cleanest first choice
- you want a more transcript-backed practice system, not just plausible advice

Pick one drill and run it for multiple reps before switching.

Also choose one matching exact move from
`analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` so the drill stays
behavior-specific.

If you want shorter, move-specific drill cards before using the full drill map,
also use:

- `analysis/speaker-pattern-drill-cards.md`

## Drill 1: Stay-in-character pause recovery

Use when:

- you blank
- interruptions break your flow
- you look less confident than you actually are

Primary evidence:

- `Evidence 1: Confidence as visible recovery, not perfect composure`
- `Evidence 3: Rehearsal quality determines live performance quality`

Main source sessions:

- `Ultraspeaking #19 How to be perceived as more confident`
- `Vinh Giang #17 How to Speak When All Eyes Are On You...`

Why this drill is justified:

- the transcript-backed claim is not that strong speakers never wobble
- it is that they recover without leaking panic
- this drill directly trains that visible recovery behavior

The behavior it targets:

- panic after losing your place
- apologizing into collapse
- visible break in composure

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 15: Rehearse The Pressure Shape, Not Just The Content`

## Drill 12: Next-step persuasion reps

Use when:

- you pitch too hard for conversion
- objections make you over-explain
- your recommendation has no clear next move

Primary evidence:

- `Evidence 21: The goal of a pitch is the next step, not immediate conversion`
- `Evidence 29: Objection-heavy conversations improve when friction is mapped in advance`

Main source sessions:

- `Think Fast Talk Smart #9 How to Pitch Anything: 10 20 30 Rule by Guy Kawasaki`
- `Vinh Giang #7 How to Think 10 Steps Ahead In Any High Stakes Conversation`

Why this drill is justified:

- the evidence supports progression over over-conversion
- it also supports anticipating friction instead of improvising blindly
- this drill directly builds those two habits

The behavior it targets:

- pitching as all-or-nothing conversion
- friction blindness
- vague or missing next-step asks
