# Speaking Progress Scorecard

Baseline and re-test scorecard for measuring whether speaking practice is
actually changing visible behavior.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The atlas now helps learners:

- diagnose the right problem
- choose a pathway
- run focused weekly actions
- execute a seven-day sprint

What it still needed was a simple measurement layer:

- how to tell whether speaking actually improved

This scorecard is not a scientific instrument. It is a practical self-review
tool for comparing:

- before practice
- after one week
- after one month

The goal is not perfect precision. The goal is to stop relying on vague
feelings like:

- “I think I’m getting better”
- “That felt bad so it must have been bad”

If you know the weak dimension but not the exact replacement move, pair this
file with `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` so the
next practice block is anchored in one named transcript-backed behavior.

## How to use this scorecard

1. Record one short speaking sample before practice.
2. Score yourself quickly across the dimensions below.
3. Run one pathway, sprint, or focused action block for at least one week.
4. Record a second sample using a similar task.
5. Score again and compare only the categories you were intentionally training.
6. Choose one exact move from
   `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` for the next
   cycle.

Good sample types:

- a 60-second work update
- an answer to one hard question
- a short explanation of something technical
- a one-minute story or personal anecdote

## Scoring rule

Use a simple 1 to 5 scale.

### 1

- severe weakness
- this issue visibly hurts the communication

### 2

- weak
- the problem is frequent and obvious

### 3

- functional but inconsistent
- the communication works, but the weakness is still noticeable

### 4

- strong
- the skill is mostly visible and reliable

### 5

- unusually strong for an ordinary speaker
- this dimension actively helps the communication

## Core scorecard

### 1. Composure under attention

Question:

- When attention lands on me, do I still look and sound under control?

Look for:

- whether you rush the first sentence
- whether you panic when you lose your place
- whether pause reads like control or fear

### 2. Clarity and structure

Question:

- Does the listener get the point quickly without digging through my wording?

Look for:

- whether the answer arrives early

- whether extra detail helps or only delays the point

### 3. Delivery aliveness

Question:

- Does my speech have enough variation, emphasis, and pace shape to hold attention?

- Do I sound clean, credible, and useful in professional settings?

- Do I sound interested, human, and easier to connect with?

Look for:

Question:

## How to interpret changes

### If one score rises and the rest stay flat

- that is normal
- it usually means your practice was specific enough to work

### If all scores stay flat

Possible causes:

- you changed tasks too often
- you practiced too little
- you trained the wrong weakness first
- you relied on reading rather than repetition

Next move:

- go back to `analysis/speaking-diagnosis-guide.md`
- choose one pathway or sprint only
- then choose one item from
  `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`

### If the score improved but it still feels awkward

- trust the visible behavior shift first
- comfort often lags behind visible competence

### If you got louder or faster but not better

- you may be training intensity instead of clarity, presence, or timing

Next move:

- return to the concept linked to the weak dimension
- and pick the matching exact approach from
  `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`

## Best times to use this scorecard

- before starting the starter curriculum
- before and after a weekly practice sprint
- at the start and end of a business communication block
- when you feel stuck and need proof of what is or is not changing
