# Roleplay Feedback Scorecard

Simple scoring sheet for evaluating multi-turn speaking roleplays and realistic
practice exchanges.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The repo already has:

- `analysis/speaking-roleplay-packs.md`
- `analysis/real-life-speaking-simulations.md`
- `analysis/listener-feedback-template.md`
- `analysis/speaking-progress-scorecard.md`

This file adds a narrower evaluation layer:

- how to judge whether the first answer was strong
- how to judge whether the second turn still held up under pressure

That matters because many learners sound decent on the first sentence and then
lose shape as soon as the room pushes back.

If you know what failed in the roleplay but not the exact replacement move,
pair this file with `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`
so the next rep is built around a named transcript-backed behavior.

## When to use this

Use this after:

- running one roleplay pack
- doing a recorded multi-turn interview or meeting rep
- practicing disagreement, Q&A, or explanation under follow-up pressure

If another person is facilitating the rep, also use:

- `analysis/partner-roleplay-facilitator-guide.md`

If you want a stronger before-you-score comparison point for how the answer
could sound in a real room, also use:

- `analysis/real-world-speaking-application-examples.md`

## How to use it

1. Run one roleplay pack or multi-turn scenario.
2. Record it if possible.
3. Score only the most relevant categories.
4. Pick one weak category for the next rep.
5. Re-run the same scenario before switching to a different one.
6. Choose one exact move from
   `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` for the next
   rep.

## Five core categories

Score each 1 to 5.

### 1. First-sentence clarity

Question:

- Did the first answer get to the point fast enough?

Low score signs:

- throat-clearing
- backstory before answer
- visible verbal scrambling

High score signs:

- the point arrives quickly
- the listener knows what the answer is early
- the opening sounds chosen, not accidental

### 2. Pressure composure

Question:

- Did the speaker stay steady when challenged or interrupted?

Low score signs:

- speed spike
- apology clutter
- obvious panic energy

High score signs:

- pause stays usable
- tone remains controlled
- pressure does not destroy shape

### 3. Second-turn resilience

Question:

- Did the answer stay useful after the follow-up question got sharper?

Low score signs:

- the second answer collapses into defensiveness
- the speaker loses the original point
- the follow-up gets answered with fog

High score signs:

- the second turn still has structure
- the speaker responds to the real concern
- the answer becomes clearer instead of messier

### 4. Credibility and usefulness

Question:

- Did the answer sound like it helps the listener act or understand?

Low score signs:

- vague abstractions
- dodging
- lots of words with no decision or next step

High score signs:

- concrete reason or example
- visible ownership
- clear next step when needed

### 5. Warmth or relational control

Question:

- Did the speaker manage the relationship well, especially in social or tense moments?

Low score signs:

- sharpness
- coldness
- performative humor
- awkward recovery that makes things worse

High score signs:

- acknowledgment before disagreement
- curiosity that feels real
- humor that fits the moment
- repair that is brief and calm

## What to compare across reps

If you run the same roleplay twice, compare:

- did the first answer get shorter and clearer?
- did the second turn stay calmer?
- did the answer defend less and respond more?
- did the listener have less work to do?

Before scoring the next rep, it can also help to open one matching item from
`analysis/real-world-speaking-application-examples.md` so the speaker and
facilitator both have a stronger concrete reference for what the better version
could sound like.
