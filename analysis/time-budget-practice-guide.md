# Time-Budget Practice Guide

Practice routes for learners with different daily time budgets.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The atlas now gives learners:

- first-session entry
- one-week sprints
- month-one structure
- diagnosis and review loops

What it still needed was a time-budget layer.

Many learners do not ask:

- what is the best pathway?

They ask:

- what can I actually do if I only have 10 minutes today?

This file makes the atlas more realistic by translating the current practice
system into time-constrained versions.

If you know the time budget but not the exact move to train inside it, pair
this file with `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` so
the schedule serves one named behavior instead of a broad intention.

## How to use this guide

Pick the daily budget that matches real life, not the ideal version of you.

Before choosing the drills, choose one exact move from
`analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`.

It is better to run:

- 10 honest minutes per day for two weeks

than:

- one big session followed by nothing

## Track 1: 10 minutes a day

Best for:

- very busy learners
- people rebuilding consistency
- anyone likely to quit if the plan feels too large

What to do:

1. Spend 2 minutes reviewing your current focus sentence.
2. Spend 4 minutes doing one drill or one tiny rep.
3. Spend 2 minutes recording or re-recording one short answer or update.
4. Spend 2 minutes noting what changed visibly.

Best fits:

- `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`
  Start with one move only.
- `analysis/focused-improvement-actions.md`
- `analysis/weekly-practice-sprints.md`
  but only one micro-rep from the sprint each day

Best drills for this track:

- `pressure-question five-second reset`
- `pause replacement reps`
- `small-talk entry reps`
- one PREP rep

Do not do:

- multiple drills in one day
- full multi-concept study sessions

## Track 2: 20 minutes a day

Best for:

- most working professionals
- learners who can practice consistently but not deeply every day

What to do:

1. Spend 3 minutes reviewing the current problem and the previous note.
2. Spend 8 minutes on one focused drill or one sprint component.
3. Spend 5 minutes recording a sample or answering one prompt.
4. Spend 4 minutes reviewing the result and writing one adjustment note.

Best fits:

- `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`
  Keep the same move all week.
- `analysis/weekly-practice-sprints.md`
- `analysis/speaking-sample-prompt-bank.md`
- `analysis/speaking-progress-scorecard.md`

Best drills for this track:

- `goal-audience-data card`
- `PREP argument reps`
- `musicality contrast reps`
- `humor as connection rep`

Do not do:

- two different pathways at once
- a new prompt family every day

## Track 3: 45 minutes a day

Best for:

- serious learners
- people preparing for a high-stakes communication period
- learners running a true month-one block

What to do:

1. Spend 5 minutes reviewing the target problem and the current week’s route.
2. Spend 15 minutes on drills or sprint exercises.
3. Spend 10 minutes on one recorded sample or one prompt-family rep.
4. Spend 10 minutes scoring, reviewing, or getting outside feedback.
5. Spend 5 minutes choosing the next rep for tomorrow.

Best fits:

- `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`
  Choose one primary move and one adjacent move only if the first is stable.
- `analysis/month-one-speaking-training-plan.md`
- `analysis/business-communication-curriculum.md`
- `analysis/weekly-review-and-adjustment-template.md`

Best use of the extra time:

- deeper retest comparisons
- two passes on the same answer
- listener feedback on one sample

Do not do:

- add extra content reading just because time is available
- switch from repetition to passive browsing

## Weekly shape by time budget

### If you only have 10 minutes a day

- use one problem only
- keep the same prompt family for the whole week
- prioritize consistency over variety

### If you have 20 minutes a day

- use one pathway and one sprint
- record at least two samples during the week
- review once near the end of the week

### If you have 45 minutes a day

- run one pathway seriously
- use baseline, retest, listener feedback, and weekly review
- treat the week like a small training block rather than casual practice

## Decision rule

If you keep missing practice, do not change the pathway first.

Change the time budget first.

Common useful adjustment:

- from 45 minutes imagined to 20 real minutes
- from 20 unstable minutes to 10 consistent minutes
