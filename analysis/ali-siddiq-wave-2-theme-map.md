# Ali Siddiq Wave 2 Theme Map

First-pass theme map grounded in the 202 available transcript records.

## Main themes

### 1. Consequence-first orientation

Use a concrete problem, choice, or consequence to create forward motion before
delivering all context. Transfer to presentations by opening with a situation
the audience can picture, then supplying only the context needed for the next
beat.

Anchors: `#47`, `#151`, `#198`.

### 2. Scene detail as compression

Specific people, objects, locations, and actions can communicate several
abstract claims at once. In a presentation, choose one representative scene
before listing general findings.

Anchors: `#4`, `#56`, `#118`.

### 3. Escalation by changed conditions

Build a sequence in which each beat changes the available choices or the
audience's interpretation. The speaker's job is to make the transition between
conditions legible, not merely to increase volume or emotion.

Anchors: `#33`, `#64`, `#82`.

### 4. Character contrast and point of view

Different people want different things and interpret the same event
differently. In a talk, a controlled contrast between two viewpoints can make a
tradeoff or misunderstanding visible without requiring a long theory section.

### 5. Public repair as relationship maintenance

Correction, qualification, and a short aside can preserve trust when they are
brief and connected to the thread. This supports the recovery layer of the
unified system, but should not be copied as unrestricted improvisation.

### 6. Repetition, callback, and transformed meaning

Return to a phrase, image, or premise after the audience has acquired new
information. The return earns its effect when the meaning has changed, not
when the words are merely repeated.

### 7. Difficulty made discussable through humor

Humor can lower the entry cost to family, relationship, and institutional
topics, but the transferable principle is shared recognition and specificity,
not edgy content. The workplace-safe version needs a separate risk filter.
