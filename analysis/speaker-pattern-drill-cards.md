# Speaker Pattern Drill Cards

Short training cards for the most useful speaking moves in this repo.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The repo now has:

- real speaker exemplars
- worked situation examples
- a reusable move playbook
- a context-first pattern guide
- evidence-backed drill reasoning

This file adds the missing last-mile practice layer:

- exactly how to train one move in a few minutes
- what a good rep looks like
- what to say
- where to use it in real life this week

If you want the exact transcript-backed move name before choosing a drill card,
start with `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` first.

## How to use this file

1. Pick one card only.
2. Match it to one item from
   `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md`.
3. Run the drill for 3 to 5 reps in one sitting.
4. Use the same move in one live moment within 72 hours.
5. Review only one visible behavior after the live rep.

Do not collect cards. Use them.

## Card 1: Acknowledge before you disagree

### Real speaker anchor

- Sam Altman under pressure

### Best use case

- stakeholder disagreement
- tense meeting pushback
- interview challenge question

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 19: Take A Position, Give A Reason, Then Reopen`

### Starter line

> "I see the concern. The part I read differently is..."

### Two-minute drill

1. Write three disagreement prompts.
2. Answer each one with:
   - acknowledgment
   - point of difference
   - one reason
3. Record all three in a row.

### Good rep looks like

- no immediate defensiveness
- the disagreement is specific
- the tone stays steady

### Common failure

You skip the acknowledgment and sound like you are swatting the person away.

### Live use this week

Use this once in a real meeting instead of leading with "I don't think that's
right."

## Card 2: Bottom line before backstory

### Real speaker anchor

- Guy Kawasaki
- Vinh Giang

### Best use case

- project update
- recommendation
- professional answer under time pressure

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 5: Prepare With Goal, Audience, And Constraints`
- `Approach 13: Pre-Handle Objections Before The Room Raises Them`

### Starter line

> "Quick update: we're on track, but the main risk is..."

Or:

> "My recommendation is to..."

### Two-minute drill

1. Pick one current work topic.
2. Say it once in 45 seconds.
3. Say it again in 20 seconds.
4. Force the first sentence to carry the point both times.

### Good rep looks like

- the point arrives immediately
- the listener could repeat the decision back
- background supports the point instead of hiding it

### Common failure

You keep the same rambling answer and only add "quick update" at the front.

### Live use this week

Give one real update using only:

- status
- risk
- next step

## Card 3: Honest uncertainty without collapse

### Real speaker anchor

- Sam Altman
- Matt Abrahams

### Best use case

- interview uncertainty
- executive Q&A
- data question you cannot fully answer yet

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 3: Pause, Frame, Then Answer`
- `Approach 13: Pre-Handle Objections Before The Room Raises Them`

### Starter line

> "We don't know the full answer yet. What we do know is... and the next thing I'd check is..."

### Two-minute drill

1. Write three questions you cannot fully answer.
2. For each one, answer in this order:
   - what we know
   - what we do not know
   - what happens next

### Good rep looks like

- uncertainty is owned cleanly
- one grounded fact is present
- the answer still moves forward

### Common failure

You say only what is unknown and sound evasive.

### Live use this week

Use this once when someone asks for certainty you do not yet have.

## Card 4: Summary reset after drift

### Real speaker anchor

- Ali Abdaal

### Best use case

- you start rambling
- you lose the thread mid-answer
- your explanation is getting too long

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 1: Talking-Points Navigation Instead Of Script Recital`
- `Approach 6: Compress Before You Expand`

### Starter line

> "The short version is..."

Or:

> "What actually matters here is..."

### Two-minute drill

1. Speak for 30 seconds on any topic until you drift.
2. Stop and reset with a summary line.
3. Repeat that cycle three times.

### Good rep looks like

- restart is clean
- no apology spiral
- the takeaway becomes clearer after the reset

### Common failure

You keep talking past the moment where a reset would help.

### Live use this week

The next time you hear yourself wandering, stop and use one summary reset
instead of digging deeper.

## Card 5: Visible appreciation social opener

### Real speaker anchor

- strong conversationalists from Charisma on Command

### Best use case

- networking
- meeting someone new
- breaking flat small talk

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 8: Use Curiosity To Create Better Conversations`
- `Approach 16: Start With Connection Before Content`

### Starter line

> "I've been looking forward to this. I heard you were the person who..."

### Two-minute drill

1. Write three real people you may speak with this week.
2. For each, write:
   - one specific appreciation
   - one real follow-up question
3. Say each opener out loud once.

### Good rep looks like

- appreciation is specific
- curiosity is visible
- the opener creates room for a real answer

### Common failure

You use vague praise that sounds performative.

### Live use this week

Replace one generic opener with specific appreciation plus one textured
follow-up.

## Card 6: Shared-reality humor

### Real speaker anchor

- Chris Duffy

### Best use case

- light tension in a meeting
- ordinary social conversation
- moments where a shared absurdity is obvious

Exact approach match:

- `Approach 11: Use Humor As Shared Relief, Not Performance`
- `Approach 17: Put The Turn Late And Leave Room For The Laugh`

### Starter line

> "We have somehow turned three bullets into a mythology."

### Two-minute drill

1. Write down three mildly absurd things that happened this week.
2. Turn each one into a single-line image.
3. Say the line and leave one beat after it.

### Good rep looks like

- the line comes from shared context
- it is short
- you leave space after it

### Common failure

You apologize before the joke or explain it after.

### Live use this week

Notice one shared absurdity and make one short comment about it without trying
to top the room.

## Card 7: Moment-based presentation opening

### Real speaker anchor

- Matt Abrahams
- Tristan de Montebello

### Best use case

- presentation opening
- formal talk
- important meeting kickoff

### Starter line

> "Three weeks ago, a customer got stuck before they ever reached the part we were proud of. That's why this matters."

### Two-minute drill

1. Pick one presentation topic.
2. Write one specific moment that proves why it matters.
3. Say the opening in 20 seconds.
4. Cut any decorative setup.

### Good rep looks like

- there is a concrete moment
- the relevance appears fast
- the opening sounds deliberate, not procedural

### Common failure

You tell a long story before the listener knows why it matters.

### Live use this week

Rewrite one real opening around a specific moment instead of "today I want to
talk about..."

## Card 8: First-sentence composure under pressure

### Real speaker anchor

- Sam Altman
- Matt Abrahams

### Best use case

- surprise question
- all-eyes-on-you moment
- panel or executive room

### Starter line

First: one deliberate beat.

Then:

> "The clearest answer is..."

### Two-minute drill

1. Ask yourself three sharp questions out loud.
2. Before each answer, pause one full beat.
3. Start with one clear first sentence only.

### Good rep looks like

- the pause looks deliberate
- the first sentence is cleaner
- your pace is chosen, not panic-driven

### Common failure

You either blurt or pause so long it looks like collapse.

### Live use this week

Before answering one real hard question, protect the first beat and say the
first sentence more slowly than you want to.

## Card 9: Clearer explanation under language or accent pressure

### Real speaker anchor

- multilingual clarity route in the repo

### Best use case

- second-language speaking
- fast work explanation
- clarity pressure when nerves rise

### Starter line

> "The main issue is the onboarding delay. The customer gets stuck before activation."

### Two-minute drill

1. Choose one work explanation you often give.
2. Say it once at normal speed.
3. Say it again with:
   - slower transition points
   - stronger key nouns
   - fewer fillers

### Good rep looks like

- structure becomes easier to follow
- key words become more audible
- clarity improves without fake accent performance

### Common failure

You slow every word equally and sound frozen.

### Live use this week

Use this in one explanation where clarity matters more than sounding fast.

## Card 10: Stronger follow-up question for depth

### Real speaker anchor

- strong conversationalists from Charisma on Command

### Best use case

- social conversation
- one-on-one meeting
- podcast-style curiosity practice

### Starter line

> "What ended up being harder about that than people assume?"

Or:

> "What surprised you most about that?"

### Two-minute drill

1. Write three flat questions you normally ask.
2. Rewrite each into a deeper follow-up.
3. Say the new version out loud with a slower, more interested delivery.

### Good rep looks like

- the question invites specifics
- it feels interested rather than interrogative
- the other person has somewhere real to go

### Common failure

You ask a deep-sounding question with no actual listening behind it.

### Live use this week

Use one upgraded follow-up question in a real conversation and stay with the
answer instead of switching back to yourself.
