# Speaking Sample Prompt Bank

Reusable recording prompts for baseline, retest, and deliberate speaking
practice.

Date: August 10, 2026.

## Purpose

The atlas now helps learners:

- diagnose the right problem
- choose a pathway
- run weekly sprints
- score visible progress

What it still needed was a cleaner task layer for recording comparable samples.

Without that, learners often make one baseline sample too easy and the retest
sample completely different, which makes the scorecard less useful.

This file gives the repo a stable set of speaking prompts that can be reused
for:

- baseline recording
- week-one retest
- month-one retest
- regular deliberate practice

If you already know the prompt family but not the exact move to evaluate, pair
this file with `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` so
your samples test one named behavior instead of a broad skill category.

## How to use this bank

1. Pick one prompt family that matches the skill you are trying to improve.
2. Record a baseline sample using one prompt.
3. After one week of practice, record a second sample from the same family.
4. Score both with `analysis/speaking-progress-scorecard.md`.
5. Choose one exact move from
   `analysis/transcript-backed-concrete-speaking-approaches.md` to protect
   across both recordings.

Do not switch prompt families mid-comparison unless your goal changed.

## Prompt Family 1: Pressure answers

Best for:

- freezing
- first-sentence panic
- hard-question handling

Use these for:

- `Pathway 1`
- `Pathway 5`
- the pressure and question-handling scorecard dimensions

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 2`, `Approach 3`, `Approach 15`

Prompts:

1. What is one decision you made recently that turned out to be right, and why?
2. Tell me about a time you were wrong about something important.
3. What is one piece of advice you disagree with, and why?
4. What is one weakness in your current way of working that you are trying to fix?
5. What would you do if a stakeholder strongly disagreed with your recommendation?

What to observe:

- how you start
- whether you pause before answering
- whether your answer has a visible structure

## Prompt Family 2: Clarity and explanation

Best for:

- rambling
- messy explanations
- audience-fit problems

Use these for:

- `Pathway 2`
- the clarity and structure scorecard dimension

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 1`, `Approach 5`, `Approach 6`, `Approach 14`

Prompts:

1. Explain your current work or a current project to a smart non-expert.
2. Explain a technical idea you know well in under one minute.
3. Explain a process that many people make more complicated than it needs to be.
4. Teach someone how to do one useful thing you know well.
5. Explain the difference between two ideas that people often confuse.

What to observe:

- whether the point arrives early
- whether the listener could follow the hierarchy
- whether you keep adding detail after the point is already clear

## Prompt Family 3: Work updates and recommendations

Best for:

- workplace credibility
- executive presence
- founder-style communication

Use these for:

- `Pathway 6`
- business communication curriculum
- workplace credibility scorecard dimension

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 5`, `Approach 7`, `Approach 13`, `Approach 19`

Prompts:

1. Give a 60-second update on a project that is slightly off track.
2. Recommend one change your team or company should make.
3. Explain a missed deadline without sounding evasive.
4. Summarize a problem, why it matters, and what should happen next.
5. Give a concise recommendation to someone more senior than you.

What to observe:

- whether you sound direct without being brittle
- whether you answer before over-contextualizing
- whether the next step is clear

## Prompt Family 4: Story and memorability

Best for:

- flat storytelling
- abstract delivery
- low listener engagement

Use these for:

- storytelling study
- delivery and memorability work

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 4`, `Approach 18`

Prompts:

1. Tell a short story about an unexpectedly difficult moment.
2. Tell a story about a mistake that taught you something useful.
3. Tell a story about a small event that now seems funny or revealing.
4. Describe a moment when you changed your mind.
5. Tell the story of a conversation you remember vividly.

What to observe:

- whether the story enters a specific scene
- whether the pacing creates movement
- whether the audience can picture the moment

## Prompt Family 5: Warmth and conversation entry

Best for:

- social hesitation
- awkwardness
- low warmth

Use these for:

- `Pathway 4`
- warmth and connection scorecard dimension

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 8`, `Approach 16`, `Approach 20`

Prompts:

1. Introduce yourself to someone new without using your job title first.
2. Start a conversation with someone you do not know well about something in the room.
3. Ask someone about something they care about and follow up twice.
4. Respond to the prompt: “So what have you been excited about lately?”
5. Turn a polite small-talk opener into a more human conversation.

What to observe:

- whether you sound interested instead of performative
- whether your follow-ups deepen the interaction
- whether warmth is visible in phrasing and tone

## Prompt Family 6: Humor and playfulness

Best for:

- forced humor
- mistimed humor
- quick-wittedness

Use these for:

- humor practice sprint
- humor naturalness scorecard dimension

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 11`, `Approach 17`

Prompts:

1. Retell a mildly frustrating moment and make it lighter without forcing a joke.
2. Describe a strange everyday behavior people accept as normal.
3. Tell a short story about something that went wrong in an amusing way.
4. React playfully to the prompt: “That meeting could have been an email.”
5. Build a light response to: “It’s so cold outside.”

What to observe:

- whether setup arrives before the turn
- whether the funny idea lands late enough in the sentence
- whether you leave space instead of explaining the line

## Prompt Family 7: Objection-heavy communication

Best for:

- pushback
- skeptical rooms
- high-stakes persuasion

Use these for:

- `Pathway 7`
- hard-question and high-stakes persuasion work

Best exact approaches:

- `Approach 3`, `Approach 19`, `Approach 22`

Prompts:

1. Convince a skeptical listener to try an idea they do not yet trust.
2. Respond to: “This sounds expensive and not urgent.”
3. Respond to: “Why should we believe this will work now?”
4. Recommend a change to someone who already prefers the status quo.
5. Explain what the next step should be when the room is unconvinced.

What to observe:

- whether you stay calm under skepticism
- whether you answer the real concern
- whether the goal is progress instead of over-conversion

## Best baseline bundles

If the learner wants a compact baseline set, start with one prompt from each:

1. Pressure answers
2. Clarity and explanation
3. Work updates and recommendations

If the learner is more socially focused, use:

1. Warmth and conversation entry
2. Humor and playfulness
3. Story and memorability
